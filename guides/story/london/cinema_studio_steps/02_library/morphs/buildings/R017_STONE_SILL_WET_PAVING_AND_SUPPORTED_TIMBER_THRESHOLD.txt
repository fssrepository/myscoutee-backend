MATERIAL-SPECIFIC DEFORMATION:
MINERAL RESPONSE: Dense stone and brick resist bending. Their fast initial motion is separation and shear at mortar beds or carved joints, exposing thick granular ends. Inside a changing block, the supernatural reforming zone is a slow cohesive mineral creep: corners bulge, aggregate and carving ridges migrate, and a new cross-section grows before the faces seat. Large blocks carry momentum and settle with a short heavy arrest; smaller chips follow locally. The old form loses its shape through this volume change, not by folding a wall sheet. Keep the reforming zone opaque and substantial.
TIMBER RESPONSE: Wood first bends along grain and separates at joinery; transverse loads open a split along visible fibres. Long fibres bridge the changing region while bundles spread, lengthen and pack into the new board or support. End grain rotates with the actual cut face. A temporarily compliant forming region permits strong curvature; the finished board regains stiffness. Long unsupported ends lag and rebound slightly, while heavy joints seat later. The grain path and rigid completed sections distinguish timber from flowing liquid.
