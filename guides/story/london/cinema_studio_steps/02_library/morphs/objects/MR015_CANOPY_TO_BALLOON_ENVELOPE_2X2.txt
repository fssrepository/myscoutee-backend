MATERIAL-SPECIFIC DEFORMATION:
METAL RESPONSE: Metal yields through bending, torsion and local plastic flow. Plates form stiff curved folds with visible thickness, flanges and moving specular highlights; bars neck down or thicken near their changing joints. Bolts and collars remain hard while adjoining metal reforms. Long slender members flex sooner than massive sockets and can overshoot slightly before seating. Large shape change comes from altered cross-section as well as rotation; no metallic colour painted over a soft generic blob.
CLOTH RESPONSE: Cloth has little bending stiffness and follows tension between seams. Free edges lag and form deep folds; stitch lines constrain the stretch. At a material conversion, yarn bundles reweave along the turning edge and preserve real front/back fabric faces. Upholstery is different from a loose hem: dense foam compresses slowly and rebounds underneath a skin of tensioned fabric, while its frame stays hard. Do not make cloth, foam and support metal deform at one identical speed.
FIBRE RESPONSE: A rope carries tension through twisted bundles and resists lateral spreading until the lay opens. Individual strands then splay, lag and interlace; slack ends curl while taut runs straighten between contacts. During conversion to a woven surface, the same strands flatten and lock into crossing rows. A thick knot deforms more slowly than a loose fibre and becomes a compact final junction. The final textile stays flexible, unlike a rigid slab painted with weave.
The four stills expose these different material responses through strain, sag, folded thickness, changing joints and contact shadows. In the moving film, thin flexible pieces can respond first while heavier or more viscous regions lag; phases overlap rather than all parts advancing on the same uniform clock.
