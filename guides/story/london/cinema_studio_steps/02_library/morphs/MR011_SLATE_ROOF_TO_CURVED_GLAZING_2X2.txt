TOP-LEFT — SOURCE:
The first accepted London roof retains its pitched slate planes, straight ridge, supporting ribs and eave blocks. Use a fixed elevated oblique angle showing roof thickness and both supporting walls.

TOP-RIGHT — FIRST UNFINISHED MATERIAL STATE:
Two near ribs bow upward while far ribs still follow the old pitch. Their slate courses thicken into interlocking curved plates with visible mineral cores and newly translucent edges; opaque slate remains across the far half. Connected rib cross-sections carry the unequal roof profiles.

BOTTOM-LEFT — SECOND UNFINISHED MATERIAL STATE:
The roof is broadly curved; near plates are clear thick glass while one broad far strip remains opaque slate with irregular partly glazed plate edges. The ridge has lowered into the new rib network. Eave blocks are partly carved corbels with large unfinished lobes and a continuous supporting core.

BOTTOM-RIGHT — RESOLVED DESTINATION:
All roof plates form supported curved glazing with thick fixed edges; all eaves have deep complete stone corbels. The original walls and road remain. Transparent glass permits the same interior depth to be seen; it does not expose a substitute scene or erase the structure.
