# Visual direction — an entrancing, overwhelming, transforming city

The September 9 direction is **fast camera movement, a living city, stronger effects and physically transforming buildings; the walker is almost lost inside the experience**. This direction is already included in the world, storyboard and video prompts. Do not paste this guide alongside them.

The man walks at human speed. The camera overtakes him, swings ahead, accelerates sideways, catches his gaze on glass and travels around him in short purposeful arcs. Transitions are visible changes in the existing city: hold the same wall, window or joint in view as its parts move into their new positions. Keep foreground traffic clear of that action; no wipes, hidden cuts, whip-pan reveals or dissolves concealing a replacement scene. Strong parallax gives depth; a face or gesture briefly becomes stable at the end of each surge, giving the overload recognizable human content.

Architecture actually changes: window bays slide, facades lengthen, arcades repeat and roof structures articulate into new forms. Stone, metal, glass, shadows and wet reflections follow the transformation. Bodies and faces retain their shapes. The pedestrian route stays connected; bystanders do not experience the change as a disaster.

One dominant transformation reads at a time. Continuous spinning, deformation on every plane and uninterrupted flashing would obscure the story. Memory participants act within their assigned projection or spatial emergence; the film must not become camera movement over static reference images.

Cards remain normally visible above heads and gain only a little internal fill during direct gaze. The protagonist's own card becomes readable in Block 06. No cards, architectural deformation or spectacular camera movement survive the clinical cut: stillness is the final contrast.

## Personal memory and sound

The existing rural memory fragments belong to childhood near Orosháza in the Viharsarok: paprika rows, corn rows, a flat Great Plain horizon and wind through the leaves. Keep these inside the remembered world. The original Dél-Alföld-inspired tekerő and clarinet motif carries this sense of home through the fused city, from intimate longing to controlled overload; the facade starts and settles on the motif's main accents, with synchronized stone and metal/glass foley giving the change material weight. See [sound direction](VOICE.md#music-and-transformation-foley).

Gaze and walking continuity: His gaze leads the change: as he looks toward a wall or glass surface, that very surface starts changing while he keeps walking. The camera follows his gaze without stopping the forward journey. Before one change fully settles, the next visible surface begins to move, carrying the action onward in one unbroken flow. Keep a recognizable doorway, wall edge or window frame continuously visible across each change so the space remains connected. Never reset the whole scene or restart the walk; local details may visibly return to their original state within the continuing street. The final clinical hard cut is the established endpoint of this continuous city experience.

Personal memory and reversible detail: The changing street is tied to his personal memories. At each scripted memory location, a familiar doorway, moving ornament or face in a projected memory catches his eye. A warm memory briefly softens his eyes and mouth; a painful one tightens his jaw and breath while he keeps walking. Use the existing memory beats, not additional flashbacks. Nearby people glance at each other, briefly meet his eyes, then look away; these are ordinary exchanges that he struggles to interpret, not a coordinated hostile crowd. Street ornaments participate: carved leaves curl, iron scrolls unwind and cornice details slide into new patterns. A changed detail can reverse in the time of one blink: show the same visible pieces rapidly moving back to their exact earlier arrangement while the camera remains open and continuous. The next surface can begin changing before he has understood the last one. His gaze discovers these events; he makes no commanding gesture and never appears to control them. Keep the experience human: recognition, tenderness, unease and accumulating difficulty telling the present from memory. People retain their identities and natural anatomy. In the eight-second pilot, use the existing kind Soho memory and his uneasy return to the street; do not add an unrelated painful episode merely to show both moods.



IMPOSSIBLE CITY — PHOTOGRAPHIC MATERIALS
This is a fully imagined, inhabited dream metropolis. Photorealism applies to skin, stone, rain, light and camera optics; its spatial behaviour is impossible. London and Budapest occupy the same streets at once: the listed Hungarian landmarks, London junctions and fused building fronts are physical parts of one connected world. Build rich sculpted depth with projecting limestone bays, layered iron balconies, carved foliage, tram wires and warm cultural glass. The environment must already feel extraordinary before a major transformation starts.
Across the walk, the built city continuously rearranges itself in visible space. While one facade makes a large change, a neighbouring strip of cornice or iron ornament begins a smaller delayed movement, carrying the change down the street. Solid stone keeps its grain and carved detail while joints open and close; iron remains iron as its scrolls curl. Hold a fixed ground-floor doorway or pavement seam in view to prove that the architecture itself moves. Keep one large change dominant and the moving remembered faces legible. Pedestrians recognise this world as their everyday setting; the protagonist responds with personal recognition and mounting uncertainty. In Block 07 this activity progressively stops before the clinical cut.

VISIBLE CARDS AND UNOBSTRUCTED HEADS
In the dream city, every visible pedestrian has one landscape rounded rectangular card floating above their own crown. For each nearby person, make the card about one-and-a-half head widths wide and half a head height tall, with a quarter-head-height air gap above the hair. Its pale frosted body, continuous soft edge and single darker horizontal fill bar are visible even before anyone looks at it. The card is a persistent object in the scene, not lens flare or a screen overlay; it follows the person's position and perspective without jitter. Keep enough headroom to show the complete card. Use the same design for everyone, without demographic colours, icons or ranking labels.
No open umbrellas anywhere in the current street. Nearby people have uncovered heads and wear raincoats; use fine drizzle, wet hair, dripping eaves and tyre spray for the rain. Keep faces and the air above their heads clear of signs, vehicles and foreground bodies. Stage at least two nearby pedestrians in separated sightlines so their complete heads and cards can be read together; keep them in useful focus. At a gaze exchange, only the viewed person's existing bar grows slightly, then returns to its previous length. The other person's card remains at baseline. Cards never vanish between gaze events. The protagonist's visibility follows this block's reveal rule; memory characters and people in clinical reality have no cards.


MEMORY CAN BECOME SPACE — FOLLOW THE MODE NAMED IN EACH BEAT
The current city and remembered life interpenetrate in three concrete ways. The assigned mode governs that beat; do not force every memory to stay on glass.
PROJECTION: the remembered image plays ONTO a real pane, fitted to its perspective and bounded by its frame. It is moving footage, not the optical reflection of a person in the street. Keep faces and action dominant, with droplets and ordinary reflection faintly visible. The pane and its vehicle remain physically intact.
SPATIAL EMERGENCE: begin with the recognisable source scene at the named architectural opening. The specified object or room acquires volume and extends into the current street while its original threshold remains visible. Its light falls onto current wet stone; it casts shadows and shows changing parallax as the camera passes. A balloon can rise into the city sky, a restaurant table can extend beyond a frontage, or paper can leave a remembered desk. These are three-dimensional presences, not pictures on invisible screens. Only the named participants or objects cross; do not scatter unrelated memory fragments through the shot.
MATERIAL TRANSFORMATION: an existing city object changes shape through visible intermediate positions into a form associated with that memory: ribs become canopy supports, window bays behave like filing drawers, paving opens into remembered soil. Track the same object through its change. Its old and new forms share a joint, edge or material detail that the viewer can follow.
The protagonist and camera retain a continuous path through this impossible geography; memory architecture can open around that path. His remembered self is a separate occurrence explicitly located in the remembered scene. Do not replace the current walker's face or body. Current pedestrians retain their cards; remembered people do not acquire them even when their room extends into the street. Intimate memories can stay on glass, while the explicitly named spatial beats break that boundary. The full clinical room and its final hard cut remain reserved for Block 07.
