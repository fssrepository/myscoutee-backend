MYSCOUTEE — THE PATH BETWEEN PEOPLE

A 3 minute 20 second photorealistic short: one man's continuous walk through an impossible London–Budapest city. Living fragments of travel, relationships, work and institutional encounters appear inside real wet glass. Above people's heads, restrained translucent scores expose the habit of judging a person before hearing their story.

The city is alive and transformative. The camera surges past traffic, swings around the walker and catches human gestures in windows. London masonry articulates into Pest facades, station ribs stretch, arcades repeat and glass roofs unfold. He is spellbound and increasingly overwhelmed, while ordinary pedestrians continue their lives. Rain, material weight, identity and the walking path remain coherent. At the peak of overload, small acts of kindness still matter.

Seven ordered blocks: social memory; global routes and local encounters; work and fragmented administration; police and defence; the person versus the file; human counter-memories; a final glass threshold and hard cut to ordinary clinical reality. The protagonist is conscious, not waking up. Motion drains into stillness. The final title is MYSCOUTEE / THE PATH BETWEEN PEOPLE.

English off-screen narration; its acknowledgement of AI helping hold the story together is human and personal, without a technology showcase. Institutional characters and events are fictionalized and non-identifying. Architectural landmarks establish the dream geography, not a claim about current public affairs.
