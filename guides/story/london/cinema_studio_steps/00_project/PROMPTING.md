## Continuous material transformations throughout the pack

CONTINUOUS MATERIAL TRANSFORMATION — APPLIES TO EVERY BEAT AND DEPTH:
Foreground, middle-distance and background changes obey the same process. Begin with the actual object already visible, keep a distinctive joint or corner in view, show its moving thickness and intermediate shape, then let the resulting volume remain readable while the camera continues. Every solid face stays opaque throughout. A wall opens because its pieces separate and move; a building never loses opacity to uncover another composition. No crossfade, dissolve, double exposure, ghosted duplicate building, image replacement or slideshow transition. Ordinary transparent glass has real thickness. Where the beat fractures it, track the same visible pieces into their new solid forms; elsewhere retain its frame attachments. Glass is never a screen for a replacement scene.
For brick-to-room changes, open the existing mortar joints one course after the next. Individual bricks slide along their courses and turn at the corner, exposing their narrow side faces; their adjoining faces broaden and join into the new side wall or door reveal. Track one chipped brick edge until it becomes a corner of the new frame. Stone-to-plaster or wood changes travel over these same moving surfaces: joints close, grain develops and thickness remains, without fading one texture plane over another. Removed blocks must remain visible in the evolving wall, sill, floor or cornice; none evaporates into fog or invisible particles. Timber slats lengthen at their visible joints, iron bars bend from their existing ends and glass panes curve from their attached corners. Give every motion a visible destination and moving contact shadow. Wild changes in scale remain allowed.
An opening and the remembered encounter it exposes occupy the SAME local structure. Through the first growing gap, show part of the relevant person's face, sleeve or moving hand beside a recognisable source object. Keep that part continuously visible as moving masonry and the camera angle expose the same complete person, furniture and supporting floor. Approach that exact opening without jumping to another facade or an interior camera. The floor may carry the occupied furniture outward into the street. Bodies never replace current pedestrians or turn into another memory's cast. As the next encounter develops farther along the structure, retain the earlier people in their own receding space.
Leave a memory by travelling past its still-occupied volume while its outer materials continue into the next structure. Keep the shared kerb, rail, floor edge or pier visible between encounters. A reversal retraces the material's path at speed; it does not restore an earlier frame. Music accents and source-video boundaries do not trigger an edit or a new establishing shot. Native continuation inherits the actual last geometry, object trajectories and camera velocity. The scripted final clinical HARD CUT and final title cut remain explicit cuts; neither uses a dissolve.

World and memory source prompts provide solid geometry and identity evidence; storyboard cells depict selected physical states, never ghosted temporal overlays. Exact spoken narration and ordinary character/clinical identity references are not transformation instructions. A written revision is not a rendered result and does not launch or schedule another generation.

## Choosing how places connect

CHOOSE THE CONNECTION — MATCHED MATERIAL OR CONTINUOUS CAMERA TRAVEL:
Use a material transformation where source and destination have readable corresponding parts: dome into dome, tower shaft into tower shaft, portico columns into portico columns, arch into arch, roof rib into roof rib, or an existing furniture joint into its new function. Keep those actual parts visible while they change and preserve the original support or threshold. Precision means a traceable change of form, not a mandatory brick-by-brick effect.
Where the next space has no convincing local material correspondence, preserve the current room or object and carry the camera rapidly through an already-visible opening, around its solid corner or alongside a moving vehicle into the connected next space. Show the destination through the opening before the pass, and retain a shared floor edge or other spatial anchor. The camera can ease for a human gesture, accelerate during this passage and ease again for the next recognisable form. Do not invent a forced object mutation solely to leave a scene. Neither method permits a dissolve, double exposure, hidden cut or replacement background. A moving object may partially occlude the view naturally, but the same surrounding world continues behind it.
For whole-building spectacle, prioritize named historic landmarks with recognisable silhouettes and comparable architectural organization. Establish the specific source, transform its corresponding attached parts, then give the specific destination a readable interval. In this film St Paul's and St Stephen's share the dome/two-tower/entrance structure used for the transformation; they remain distinct recognisable buildings. Do not infer an actual historical relationship from their visual correspondence. Anonymous glass boxes and generic cream-stone office blocks cannot substitute for these named landmarks. Office rooms remain valid settings for their human stories, and modern landmarks remain useful where specifically named; they do not replace the principal historic transformation merely because their geometry is simpler.

STORY PRIORITY — VISIBLE NUMERICAL RATINGS:
The ratings are the central social fact of the current city and must be visibly present with its people. Each current pedestrian has one warm-white landscape card floating a small clear gap above their own uncovered crown, with a bold dark decimal rating as its dominant content. Feature two distinct current pedestrians with readable 6.2 and 9.2 cards during the nearest camera pass; preserve each person-number association and keep the whole card in frame. Hold their digits in useful focus long enough to read at the preview resolution, with contrasting clear background and no object between the lens and the card. At greater distance the same cards remain visible and attached to their owners; the nearest featured values must be legible before the camera rises away. When architecture, traffic or framing compete with a featured score, give the score the clear sightline while the surrounding motion continues. Blank tiles, vague grey rectangles and fill bars alone do not communicate a rating. The protagonist's own score stays unreadable until the scripted Block 06 reveal. Remembered and clinical people have no cards; retain the scripted final departure from the rated city.

## Give every material change visual source and destination evidence

For each transforming object, write a concrete correspondence: source object and visible part → intermediate deformation of that same part → destination object and function. Pair this with the actual camera path that exposes its thickness, moving joints and occlusions. Support the description with the accepted source/target object images and, where useful, one solid intermediate state. Apply this to small furniture, glass, lighting, signs, street surfaces, transport components and memory objects as well as complete historic buildings. Follow the object inventory in REFERENCES.md.

Example from the pilot extension: the narrow fanlight's iron intersection remains visible as its bars thicken and curve outward into a canopy rib; each attached glass corner travels with that rib, spreading the pane into a roof segment. The camera rises beside the same outer edge, successively exposing underside, side and upper surface. A source fanlight reference and destination canopy detail specify those forms; a matching intermediate view clarifies how they remain connected. The video still has to show their continuous travel.

References supply the geometry, material texture and object identity; the written prompt supplies transformation order, duration, attachment, camera motion and the continuing human action. Preserve both kinds of detail. An ordinary cup or the accepted occupied chair can remain the same object while moving through the transforming space. Providing it with a reference does not request an additional mutation or an extra shot. Keep the central numerical rating cards legible throughout the current-city portions.

MATERIAL MORPH — CHANGE THE VOLUME AND SUBSTANCE, NOT ONLY ITS POSITION:
Show the same object changing cross-section, curvature, surface structure and physical function in full depth. Opening an unchanged window, rotating an intact wall panel or revealing a finished room is not sufficient by itself. Masonry keeps visible thick side faces, granular fractures, deep joints and weight-bearing contact throughout its deformation. It never becomes a thin flexible paper facade. For every local change, identify an original edge or surface mark, show that same part acquiring its intermediate volume and material, and follow it into the destination. If a surface fractures, show the pieces separate along the actual cracks, rotate with visible thickness and re-form into specified new parts; retain the identifiable pieces throughout rather than dissolving them into a particle cloud. Match texture, specular response, contact shadows and material sound to that changing substance. A scripted paper object may change into another material, but must visibly acquire its new thickness and solid structure before it functions as architecture. Do not rewrite the document story to solve an architectural rendering defect.

VARIABLE CAMERA PACE WITH CONTINUOUS WORLD MOTION:
Decelerate BEFORE the featured material begins its main change, close enough to read the actual joint, fracture edge and changing cross-section. While that change develops, approach along a smooth partial orbit around the changing object itself, not automatically around the walker. Expose its front, then an oblique side and, where the geometry permits, its underside; keep the same distinctive material mark and one grounded support in view through those angles. The changing form stays large and sharp during this slower travelling arc. It is a translation through space with parallax, not a fixed-position pan, digital zoom or full revolution that loses the action. A human gesture receives the same readable pace.
Once the important intermediate shapes and resulting form have been seen, accelerate through the already-visible open space toward the next event. Carry the same trajectory through an open passage or alongside a vehicle, and brake smoothly before the next featured morph so its beginning is already in view. In a large square the camera can use a longer fast approach; near a transforming surface use the slower curved inspection. Let secondary changes continue around the route without stopping to isolate every detail. Keep people, traffic and material motion at their own continuous speeds: only camera velocity changes. No footage speed ramp, freeze, jump zoom, viewpoint reset or edit. Choose these phases around this block's actual geometry and encounters, without copying the pilot itinerary into every block.

INDEPENDENT LIFE THROUGHOUT THE CITY:
The world has its own restless agency. Existing objects can awaken and change function as well as material: a metal statue steps off its plinth, a lamp stem curves to turn its light toward a passer-by, or a wooden chair moves its legs and draws itself back beside a table. Give each action a visible physical beginning and follow its original surfaces through the movement. Metal remains metal, carved stone retains grain and thickness, and timber retains its joints and grain while behaving impossibly. These events need not wait for the protagonist's gaze. Use the objects already present in each beat; stagger their rhythms, let one small detail reverse while a larger change continues, and keep them in the same camera journey. They are discoveries within an inhabited world, not separate inserts or a compulsory sequence of identical tricks in every block. Keep the featured morph and numerical rating cards clearly readable. The final block's scripted loss of motion and clinical reality still bring this activity to rest.
