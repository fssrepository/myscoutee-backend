Keep the building morphs running at their own rhythms during the spatial opening: brick courses become carved-stone arch blocks, window bays deepen into inhabited loggias and ironwork reshapes into stairs. Opening the street never substitutes for those material changes. A comparison between famous London and Budapest streets illustrates the possibility, not a compulsory named street-to-street replacement. Preserve the scripted people and keep numerical cards attached to current pedestrians.

## Continuous material transformations throughout the pack

CONTINUOUS MATERIAL TRANSFORMATION — APPLIES TO EVERY BEAT AND DEPTH:
Foreground, middle-distance and background changes obey the same process. Begin with the actual object already visible, keep a distinctive joint or corner in view, show its moving thickness and intermediate shape, then let the resulting volume remain readable while the camera continues. Every solid face stays opaque throughout. A wall opens because its pieces separate and move; a building never loses opacity to uncover another composition. No crossfade, dissolve, double exposure, ghosted duplicate building, image replacement or slideshow transition. Ordinary transparent glass has real thickness. Where the beat fractures it, track the same visible pieces into their new solid forms; elsewhere retain its frame attachments. Glass is never a screen for a replacement scene.
For brick-to-room changes, open the existing mortar joints one course after the next. Individual bricks slide along their courses and turn at the corner, exposing their narrow side faces; their adjoining faces broaden and join into the new side wall or door reveal. Track one chipped brick edge until it becomes a corner of the new frame. Stone-to-plaster or wood changes travel over these same moving surfaces: joints close, grain develops and thickness remains, without fading one texture plane over another. Removed blocks must remain visible in the evolving wall, sill, floor or cornice; none evaporates into fog or invisible particles. Timber slats lengthen at their visible joints, iron bars bend from their existing ends and glass panes curve from their attached corners. Give every motion a visible destination and moving contact shadow. Wild changes in scale remain allowed.
An opening and the remembered encounter it exposes occupy the SAME local structure. Through the first growing gap, show part of the relevant person's face, sleeve or moving hand beside a recognisable source object. Keep that part continuously visible as moving masonry and the camera angle expose the same complete person, furniture and supporting floor. Approach that exact opening without jumping to another facade or an interior camera. The floor may carry the occupied furniture outward into the street. Bodies never replace current pedestrians or turn into another memory's cast. As the next encounter develops farther along the structure, retain the earlier people in their own receding space.
Leave a memory by travelling past its still-occupied volume while its outer materials continue into the next structure. Keep the shared kerb, rail, floor edge or pier visible between encounters. A reversal retraces the material's path at speed; it does not restore an earlier frame. Music accents and source-video boundaries do not trigger an edit or a new establishing shot. Native continuation inherits the actual last geometry, object trajectories and camera velocity. The scripted final clinical HARD CUT and final title cut remain explicit cuts; neither uses a dissolve.

World and memory source prompts provide solid geometry and identity evidence; storyboard cells depict selected physical states, never ghosted temporal overlays. Exact spoken narration and ordinary character/clinical identity references are not transformation instructions. A written revision is not a rendered result and does not launch or schedule another generation.
