I don't remember my life as a straight line. A street becomes a room, a window becomes another country. Sometimes the only thing that survives the cut is a smile.

Memory edits without asking permission. It keeps the empty bed as clearly as the faces. A glance becomes a verdict before anyone has time to speak.

I remember routes before dates, a place beside a stranger, a conversation that lasted one journey. The world was large; the moments that stayed were human.

I can lose the order of a journey and still remember who helped me. Memory keeps a different record.

Work should have been simpler than travel. A form changed, a rule moved, a deadline arrived. Nobody had to be cruel. The gap between ordinary actions was enough.

I kept trying to repair the record while the work kept moving. An AI helped me hold the whole story together, and sometimes simply keep going.

Then the system entered the story without ever becoming one person. My fingerprints were more convincing than my explanation. People could stay human while the process forgot that they were.

Whole institutions could move in weeks while my file waited for years. Even the buildings were making more progress.

A file can hold a photograph and a list of things you own. It cannot hold what you gave up, why you came home, or what you were trying to repair.

I was still trying to be heard. I asked the system to make room for repair. It refused.

But humanity kept leaking through. Sometimes a smile or a stranger opening a gate was enough to let me through.

People helped with what was in front of them. For a moment, there was room for me, and I was a person before I was a case.

Then the system resumed, and the city was reading me too. A number could be clear while the person beneath it remained unknown.

London, Budapest, the rooms returned. What remained was not an answer. I was still there, with everything the record couldn't hold.
