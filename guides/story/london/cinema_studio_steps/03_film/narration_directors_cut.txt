I don't remember my life as a straight line. I remember places opening into other places, and people I thought I'd left behind.

Sometimes I remember being alone more clearly than where I was. Sometimes a face returns, and I remember what I couldn't say.

Travelling meant trusting people I might never see again. Someone made space beside them, and for a little while I belonged there too.

What stayed was often small: an awkward step, an offered hand. I couldn't have told you then which moments I would keep returning to.

At work, I wanted to be useful. I thought the papers would help explain what I knew, what I'd done, what I could offer.

Later, I kept trying to put everything in order. An AI helped me hold the whole story together, and sometimes simply keep going. I still needed people to hear it.

There were questions, searches, things to account for. I tried to answer carefully. The person across the desk could only see part of me.

Afterwards, I would go over it again. What had I said? What had they understood? Outside, people were getting on with their day. I wanted to do the same.

A photograph could show my face. A file could put my name beside it. Explaining how I'd arrived there was harder.

I wanted the record put right. Every time I explained, I had to decide where to begin, and how much someone would listen to.

Then I remember someone smiling. Someone helping me through. For a moment, there was nothing I had to explain.

People helped with what was in front of them. A hand, a cup, a place at the table. I remember being welcome.

I have a number here too. I wonder what anyone can really tell from it.

London, Budapest, rooms I thought I'd left behind. They keep coming back. I recognise them. I still don't know where this leaves me.
