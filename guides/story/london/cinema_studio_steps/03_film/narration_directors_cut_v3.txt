I don't remember my life as a straight line. Memory edits without asking permission. A glance becomes a verdict before anyone has time to speak.

A street becomes a room, a window becomes another country. Sometimes the only thing that survives the cut is a smile.

I remember routes before dates. A place beside a stranger, a conversation that lasted one journey. The world was large; the moments that stayed were human.

I can lose the order of a journey and still remember who helped me. Memory keeps a different record.

Work should have been simpler than travel. A form changed, a rule moved, a deadline arrived. Nobody had to be cruel. The gap between ordinary actions was enough.

I kept trying to repair the record while the work kept moving. An AI helped me hold the whole story together, and sometimes simply keep going.

Then the system entered the story without ever becoming one person. People could stay human while the process forgot that they were.

Whole institutions could move in weeks. My file could wait for years. Even the chairs above it changed. I was still trying to be heard.

A file can hold a photograph, a fingerprint, a signature, a list of things you own. It cannot hold what you gave up.

Or why you came home. Or what you were trying to repair. I asked the system to make room for repair. It refused.

But humanity kept leaking through. Someone smiled in the wrong room. A stranger opened a gate.

For a second, each role became a person again. A hand, a cup, a place at the table. Then the system resumed.

By then the city was reading me too. A number could be clear while the person beneath it remained unknown.

London, Budapest, every memory stayed in the glass. What remained was not an answer. I was still there, with everything the record couldn't hold.
