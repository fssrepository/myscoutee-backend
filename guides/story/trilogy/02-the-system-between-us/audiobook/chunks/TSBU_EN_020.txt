I cannot call the experience that produced this work good. I also cannot erase the person formed through it. What I make from it remains mine.

All of this happened with the possibility of imprisonment in the background. While a report described damaged judgment and a court waited for an expert who was unavailable, I completed systems, preserved testable research and discussed Navier–Stokes mathematics with a researcher. I did not spend those months waiting to be classified.

In roughly five months, the work crossed four desks that were really one. AI helped me defend the legal record. MyScoutee became an integrated product. Civic Access tried to make public administration more understandable and worthy of acceptance. The books and film required me to step outside myself far enough to direct the person I had been. The mathematical laboratory kept asking whether an idea could survive an honest attempt to break it.

The human mind cannot be caged. A court may place imprisonment in a person's future. The shadow is real. It still cannot decide where thought is allowed to travel before that future arrives.

That is the character the procedural file could not explain. The shadow did not vanish. I learned to work inside it until, on many days, I no longer felt it.

The trial court transmitted my constitutional complaint. It carried two questions already present in my defence. Could a public system that made password sharing the practical route be reconciled with European data protection? And what could adequate preparation time mean when a livelihood tax was rewritten at speed?

The Constitutional Court wrote back on 21 October 2025 without answering either question. The criminal case was still pending, so one route was too early. The exceptional direct route had already crossed its 180-day limit, so the other was too late.

One door said too early. The other said too late.

The judges became another sequence. The first assigned judge left the case, reportedly because of illness. I later sought the exclusion of the second. A third judge rejected the request as unfounded and warned of a possible procedural fine. Even asking whether the room was impartial returned as another possible financial consequence.

The exclusion filing preserved a sentence from the hearing word for word: “What—are you asking to be sent to prison?” It came during a debate over legal classification after years without continuous effective representation. I heard not a neutral question but punishment used to close the distance between confusion and obedience.

The filing also asked for an official audio record, testimony from the accountant and tax administration, and a decision on outstanding evidence. When I called, the clerk said official recording was expensive. The clerk reacted with surprise when the apartment entered the conversation. The home my parents had provided had already changed from shelter into financial evidence.

The decision arrived by post. It rejected the exclusion request and carried a fine warning, but did not answer those practical requests.

The letter also said that every party had the right to present its position and that Strasbourg remained available. In another context, those words might have been reassuring. Here they felt like an exit sign placed beyond a minefield. I had asked the domestic process to preserve its own sound and examine its own missing evidence. The answer pointed toward a distant human rights court without first answering how the next domestic step would become trustworthy.

That was the bitter version of a right: keep walking through every local trap, then ask Europe whether the path should have been safe. The sentence did not reveal anyone's private intention. It revealed the architecture I had already learned: the route of appeal remained clearer than the route of correction.

The decision relayed the challenged judge's claim that she had listened to me conscientiously. It did not resemble the hearing I had experienced: the prison remark, the smiles, the fragments lifted from a long defence, the return to my apartment, belongings and money while the causal chain remained unheard. I later answered the court in the third person with a bitter inversion: yes, she had conscientiously defended the system failure.

The next hearing had been expected on 2 July and was moved by roughly two and a half months to 16 September. The experts had been invited but did not appear; the court referred to expert availability. The exclusion request was moving at the same time. The timing became black comedy. The system had needed only eight days after the search to turn an examination into a sweeping description of me. Years later, the expert was unavailable while that description helped keep the proceeding open. What had been fast enough to label me became too slow to test the label.
