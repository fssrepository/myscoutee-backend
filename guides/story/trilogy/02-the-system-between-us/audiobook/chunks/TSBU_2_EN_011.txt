The investigation later pulled a second sentence from that period, one I had never regarded as a threat. That spring, nearly the same figure was in the news: almost ten billion euros in Hungarian cohesion funding was being withheld behind rule-of-law conditions. I priced twenty lost years at that amount and gave the impossible invoice a crude punchline. If they could act as though anything were permitted, then, on the page at least, so could I. My operated foot still could not carry me; depression had narrowed the world to the room. From there I sent the system a bill for twenty years and the rule of law.

At one point I sent the tax administration a close photograph of the swollen, operated area itself. It was not a photograph of a bandage. The incision was visible: an intimate, ugly record of a body cut open and still trying to heal while the administrative sequence kept moving. I wrapped it in an obscene joke about using that half-cut, swollen leg to kick an institution in the backside because it was not doing its job.

The photograph placed a body cut open beside a portal field.

The same period produced an even barer line. In Hungarian, and much less politely, I wrote to the institution: “Go on, then. Fine me.” By then punishment seemed to be the one service the system might still deliver on time. No official was named. The sentence travelled past the person at the desk and into the same unnamed machinery that had kept returning after correction, payment and advance notice. Immobilized in bed, I no longer believed that another careful explanation would close it.

I had corrected the return, paid the tax, withdrawn the monetary demand, announced the operation in advance and asked for peace. Lawful repair still did not feel like an exit.

The sentence, the photograph and the joke later travelled without the bed, the stored food, the advance notice or the corrected tax return. By the time they entered the forensic account, the man lying alone and unable to walk had become a severe paranoid personality disorder reaching the level of pathological mental illness. The chair beside the bed was still empty.

Part Four — When a Person Becomes Evidence.

11. The Knock One Year Later.

On 22 November 2023, roughly a year after the first disputed sentence, police came to my home.

The bell kept ringing. Whoever stood outside did not tire. They said they were from the post. A delivery belonged to ordinary life, and in the end I opened the door to let ordinary life in.

The door came back at me.

There was no interval in which the visitors became guests. More bodies followed, women and men in bulletproof vests, until a hallway built for one visitor held the force assembled around a single sentence. Hands took my arms, the wall caught my weight, and metal closed behind my back. Then a boot found the foot that had already been cut open and put back together. Pain ran white through the narrow hall.

They took me to the police station in handcuffs and released me later that day. The injury kept repeating the moment. The nail came away three times, as if even the body had been pulled into the system's habit of beginning again.

The home in Part I had been the place my parents helped me secure, the place that stood empty while I lived in a shared London room, the lower-cost base that made independent product work possible.

Now the state had entered it disguised as the post and turned me against my own wall while it searched the rooms.

The previous day, I had submitted another police report while trying to obtain help with an interrupted private orthodontic treatment. A brace remained in my mouth and required regular adjustment, yet the treatment relationship had collapsed and no new provider wanted to inherit it. I had asked the police for help. The next morning they stood inside my home for something else.

My phone and computer were taken. Objects that had held work, memory and the unfinished MyScoutee system became evidence containers. The devices knew more of my recent life than the people entering the flat. Once removed, they also took away the easiest route through which I could reconstruct what had happened.

I had not escaped the system by hiding from lawful work. I had walked back into it by daring to work at all. Now it stood in my rooms and carried the work away.
