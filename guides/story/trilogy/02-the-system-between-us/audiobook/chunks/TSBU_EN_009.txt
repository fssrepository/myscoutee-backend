On 23 November, an ordinary newspaper article about a fresh update to the NAV system happened to cross my screen. It was not a notice and it was not about me. One detail caught against what I believed had already been closed. I opened the tax portal for a closer look.

What should have been a quick check spread across the desk. I opened my own account history and placed the accountant's submissions beside it. Actions performed through my login appeared in one trail. Her submissions and returned error messages continued in another. One trail stopped where the other began. The critical warning sat on the side I could not see.

Then the version numbers surfaced. NAV had updated the filing system while the accountant's software was still preparing the older format. A newer return existed among her files but had not crossed that technical boundary into the system. When I opened it, its calculation was wrong as well. A failure of transmission had been hiding a failure inside the form.

On 23 and 24 November, I moved between the two histories and the telephone. The administration's screen began where the accountant's trail stopped. Her files continued where my inbox ended. The same return was absent in one place, rejected in another, replaced in a third and still wrong when I calculated it. Everyone had a document that made the next sentence seem reasonable. No two documents belonged to the same moment.

She had entered my account for selected tasks, then left it again. The password had crossed the desk. The warning had not.

The surviving 13:08 call holds the sound of that hour. The filing problem had not created all the years behind it. But the official's clipped replies, which I heard as dry and cynical, landed as if the system were taking the remaining possibility of family with everything else. My voice is panicked and spent. In the middle of codes, forms and missing notices, I say, “I can no longer have a child.” It is not an argument. It is shock becoming surrender in one sentence.

Within roughly a week, I reconstructed the calculation, understood the status and reached the corrected result. The accountant had had months, three attempts and direct help from the administration. Even afterward, our exchange showed that she still did not understand the instruction in the same way I had come to understand it. I revoked the representation because the communication triangle had to be stopped before it produced another version.

The accountant was also inside a transition forced through at speed, with clients and rules moving beneath her. The reversal remained: I performed the repair for which I had retained a professional, while the legal responsibility had never left me. Pressure travelled through every participant. The final consequence did not.

Some calls from the period survive, but not the decisive exchanges behind the remarks I later recorded in my defence speech. Their shape can be inferred only indirectly—from the later writing, the surrounding calls and the steps that followed. In that later account, the remarks return one by one.

Accountants do not usually cause problems.

Why did you give her your government-portal password? Surely you can find an accountant who can keep the books without being given it.

Entrepreneurs are not usually interested in what their accountant does in their name.

You do not know even this? Do the figures match? Was there an advance invoice?

By the fourth sentence, each role had been stripped of a little dignity. The accountant could not be the problem. Entrepreneurs did not care. The client who cared should have been the accountant. The password was both the expected shortcut and my fault for surrendering it.

I had reached the institution because the delegated route had failed without my knowledge. Its questions returned responsibility to the person whom that route had kept outside the decisive information.

The circle was complete.

7. The First Public-Interest Proposal.

On 1 December, I did something different from merely complaining.

I described changes.

Send direct notice to the taxpayer when a representative files. Show the error in plain language. Let the responsible person accept or challenge the result. Do not require identity-sharing where granular permission would suffice. Record important outbound calls. Pause ordinary enforcement when a serious health event makes response temporarily impossible. Test new tax routes in parallel before abolishing the old route.

Those proposals would become more elaborate later. At the time, they were an attempt to turn pressure into design.

The proposal asked the system to preserve context. Two days later, it received one sentence from me and preserved almost nothing else as efficiently.
