That was the Kafka transformation in its purest form. A highly educated programmer-mathematician had understood in about a week what the retained professional had not resolved over several months. He had corrected the return, paid the tax, ended the representation and received a written administrative record of the repair. Eight days after the police search, he entered the forensic examination. The competence that solved the underlying problem became background; resistance to unsafe identity sharing became suspiciousness; the attempt to connect institutional failures became conspiracy; the file supplied a new character.

I contest that character.

Sometimes I played it.

If a file had already written me as pathologically mad, I felt entitled to put on the mask and push the part until its absurdity became visible. The file could call that performance another symptom. I used it as satire. I could step outside myself, exaggerate the character written around me, then return to the desk and analyse the scene.

That was another part of me the report missed.

A forensic opinion is built to answer a legal question, not to hold a life. Yet once attached to a person, its language can travel more easily than fifty countries, five London years, a mathematics degree, a repaired return or every stranger whose help made the journeys possible. The file treated those human relationships as noise around a diagnosis. Part I had shown that they were the signal.

The word paranoia became stranger with time. Institutions that had appeared immovable in 2023 were openly being replaced in 2026. That political reversal did not erase the report. It made the distance between a frightening prediction and a changing country impossible to ignore.

14. Defence by Accumulation.

The investigation and court process created another triangle: defendant, appointed defence and institution.

The triangle would not keep the same people in its corners. At the first questioning, the substitute for the appointed lawyer sent a legal assistant in his own place. The office remained unreachable, and the appointed lawyer first spoke with me only after asking to leave. Another defender was appointed on paper before I was told he existed. He was the only one who acted. Others delayed, withdrew or did not speak to me before a hearing. I eventually asked to be told when my appointed defence changed and to be given the new person's contact details.

The law required a defence chair. Continuity did not arrive with the chair.

The process often corrected a failure only after the next hearing had already collided with it. My complaint that an appointed defender would not speak to me was not resolved in time to preserve the date. By the time the defender was replaced, the hearing moved by roughly three months. Later, the forensic expert was said to be unavailable, and the calendar lost roughly another three. Each response arrived late enough to create the next delay.

The pattern felt like a wager that time would make me swallow the biography written around me. I would not trade dignity for speed. A case built around one sentence therefore entered its third year without a first-instance judgment. It did not remain inside the courtroom. Every plan began to live under its next date, and the proceeding spread across the life it claimed merely to examine.

The file I had to reconstruct.

Evidence requests were made, rejected, repeated or reframed. Partial recordings and electronic records emerged over time rather than arriving as one complete archive. The calls most important to my account were not among the surviving audio. The phone and computer remained away from me for a long period.

When I finally read the investigation material, I did not recognize a complete chronology. I saw a selection. Calls mentioned by surrounding records were missing. Electronic submissions that separated panic from repair, complaint from later satire, were not all where I expected them to be. The file had already interpreted the fragments it held. I began looking for the fragments it did not.

One absence stood out. The chronology opened on 3 December, with the sentence. What had led to it was gone.

When the phone was returned, I sat with its call history and rebuilt dates one by one. The history and correspondence showed contacts for which no audio had reached the file I saw. I requested the tax authority's recordings and copies of my own electronic submissions. When the local route did not produce them, I wrote through the central directorate. Recordings arrived in stages. A later official letter acknowledged delay. Electronic submissions followed after what the letter described as an administrative error.
