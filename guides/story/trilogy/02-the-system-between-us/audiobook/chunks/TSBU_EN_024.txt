The officer asked what I lived on, why I was not working and what the apartment said about my life, called it a bachelor flat, referred to the proposed suspended sentence and reacted with astonishment at the disputed line. A life designed to need very little was once again being read as a condition to be explained.

One constitutional task of the President is to express the unity of the nation. While Parliament prepared to choose a new person for that chair, I was moving in the opposite emotional direction. I had begun to consider whether leaving Hungary for good might also mean renouncing my Hungarian citizenship.

I had returned from London because I wanted to live in my own country. The fact that I could now imagine giving up the legal bond to it measured the distance between national unity as an office and belonging as a lived experience. I had not completed that act. The thought itself was the measure.

Exile usually means distance from home. I was living at home and felt exiled from it.

Whether the replacement deserves trust depends on how it treats the same problems when speed, power and inconvenience become its own.

Part Seven — The System We Build Instead.

26. The Product Returns.

The real face of a system is not the front page of its portal or the sentence in which it promises service. It appears when a person says: the record is wrong; the professional route failed; my body cannot meet your timetable; I need somebody to carry the context from this room into the next one.

By then, the state had begun to resemble the worst kind of relationship.

It had not been beside me while I searched for work, built independence abroad, returned home, developed the product, secured the Belgian contract, repaired the tax return or recovered alone from surgery. It appeared at the fracture. It took the sentence, the devices, the fingerprints, the expert label and the financial inventory. It claimed authority over the bad while contributing almost nothing to the good. The relationship did not support. It extracted.

The danger did not require a secret conspiracy. It was already present in the architecture: responsibility without shared information, delegated action without safe identity, accusation without complete context, expertise without a whole biography, delay without an ending and financial scrutiny before the systemic cause had been answered. Built that way, a system could humiliate a person, turn his own history against him, threaten the assets that allowed him to survive and make departure feel safer than belonging. It could do this to anyone unlucky enough to enter the same sequence.

For years, MyScoutee had asked how the natural first distrust between strangers could soften without reducing either person to a verdict. It tried to create enough private, reciprocal context for a real meeting to change the first frame.

The legal story returned the question in a harsher form. The institution classified first, and the whole person then had to fight his way back into the record. Here the two lines met.

What should a system preserve before it classifies a person?

Direct notice. Visible actions. Consent that can be withdrawn. A record of who did what. A route to correction. Private context rather than public humiliation. Reciprocal evidence rather than one unanswerable score. Enough human space for the next meeting to change the meaning of the previous one.

A healthy relationship is present in the good as well as the bad. A legitimate system should meet the same minimum test. It cannot abandon the person during work, illness and repair, then arrive only to punish the breaking point and claim the life built without it.

MyScoutee cannot repair a tax authority, court or constitution. A social product also has its own capacity for harm. Ratings can become gossip. Automation can become coercion. Matching can hide a judgment inside a number. Local operators can become unaccountable if the shared core provides no meaningful exit.

The lesson of Part II is therefore not that my product already contains the answer.

It is that the product must survive the question.

27. Humour Is a Human Audit Trail.

There is one quality no event log can fully capture.

In rooms where my future was being discussed, I sometimes made people smile. The police uniform remained. The judge remained the judge. I remained the defendant. Nothing procedural changed.

Yet the smile recorded that the roles were not the whole people.

Humour was not disrespect for the seriousness of the case. It was an informal audit trail of humanity: proof only that, for one second, several people could see the absurdity together.

The word was not added years later to make the story more literary. In one 2024 submission, I wrote that the frightening part was in the absurdity. In another, I wrote that if everything had worked lawfully, I believed nothing would have happened.
