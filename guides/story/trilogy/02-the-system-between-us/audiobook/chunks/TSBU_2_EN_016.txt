Among the recovered papers was the tax authority's complaint-handling notice dated 18 December. It recorded in writing that I had settled everything. The forensic opinion placed pathological mental illness from 3 December onward. I laid the two documents side by side.

I sent the recovered material to the police. Only then did the police request material from the tax authority again through its own route. The recordings had not been imaginary; the surrounding record already pointed toward them. The investigation waited until the person under investigation had done the first round of evidence collection for it.

It was a private investigation into the investigation, conducted without a private investigator. The local directorate that had received the charged line also held part of the context through which that line could be understood. The institution could preserve my words as evidence while leaving me to recover its own calls, routes and missing pages.

No stable defender assembled the missing chain for me. I wrote because every short institutional summary removed another cause: the abrupt law, the failed delegation, the notices I did not see, the repair, the paid tax, the operation and the request for peace. Accumulation became the only defence available against reduction.

Learning the doors.

I also had to learn the machinery through which the accumulation could enter. I learned the court's electronic filing route, called police officers, prosecutors and the court clerk, and sometimes had to identify the person or office currently carrying the case before I could ask what had happened. I complained through court administration, the prosecution hierarchy, investigative-prosecution routes and the fundamental-rights office. The papers moved more reliably than responsibility did. One office explained why another office had competence; the next explained the complaint, appeal or court route that remained available after the present door closed.

No one needed to say that a rule could be broken now because I could sue later. The sequence expressed the idea more quietly. An unanswered request became a right to complain about the missing answer. A rejected complaint became a right to challenge the rejection. A domestic gap became a road toward Strasbourg. The future remedy was repeatedly easier to describe than the present repair.

The expanding archive could itself be made to look like the problem. Hundreds of submissions from one person can resemble fixation when the reader does not ask why the same facts had to be carried from office to office. Each new filing was the shadow of a missing relationship: the official who had not called, the lawyer who had not answered, the recording that had not been requested, the notice that had not reached both sides, the earlier explanation that the next summary had removed. Repetition was not proof that every interpretation was correct. It was what one person produced when no institution preserved the whole chain for him.

Defence strategy became public-interest proposal. Complaint became system map. Closing statement became impact study. I divided responsibility into percentages, then learned that percentages can create a false precision around a human chain.

I could do this only by making myself both subject and analyst. Stepping outside my own account did not remove responsibility for my words. It let me ask the harder question: what did each person see, what could each person change, and where did the consequence go when that person passed it onward? The same habit that had once turned software into systems and journeys into routes now turned my own life into a map I could inspect.

By October 2025, I had given the accumulation a deliberately inverted title: Public-Interest Action.

Its cover reversed the room. The Hungarian State stood where the accused would normally stand. I appeared where the accusation would be represented. Inside, five strands of the experience were placed beside one another: abrupt lawmaking, data and notification, the design of the public portal, criminal procedure and moral redress.

The last proposal was almost circular. Turn the story into a film fund, let the film carry a civic lesson, then use the proceeds to restore land and leave behind a line about the moment when an individual's failure becomes the mirror of a system's failure.

The case was already trying to become the film I am now preparing.
