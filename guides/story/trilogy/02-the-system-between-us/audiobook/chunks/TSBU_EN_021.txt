Those were three roles, not one: the judge who left, the judge I challenged and the judge who ruled on the challenge. From my side of the table, the substance had still not been answered; the route kept changing its custodian.

My wider Drive now contains more than four gigabytes of material: the residue left when a person without a stable professional guide tries to make every missing step impossible to ignore.

AI gave me speed.

It did not give me representation.

The institutions retained legal power over me. Moral legitimacy was a different question. A system that had excluded me from ordinary work, failed to protect the rare route through which I returned, transformed the repair into a diagnosis and then allowed the case to remain unresolved for years could still compel my attendance. It could no longer expect its judgment to arrive with automatic moral authority.

16. The House as a Human Archive.

Before the state appeared at my Budapest door, I had already seen a Hungarian obligation cross a border and occupy a life in London.

The shared Hungarian house had contained an earlier version of the same national argument.

At its busiest, ten adults lived through one bathroom, one separate toilet and rooms never designed for that many independent lives. We had not left Hungary for one reason. Some wanted language, some work, some adventure, some escape, some a second chance. Some no longer wanted to return even when the material conditions looked worse from outside.

One housemate carried a foreign-currency mortgage behind him.

He tried to service the loan and public debts from London. Returning did not feel possible. Separation stretched. His spouse worked inside the tax administration while she and their young child remained in Hungary, and the family broke apart. He had crossed a border, but the old signature crossed with him.

His name remains private. What remains public is the shape: a financial product outlived the moment in which it was sold. He carried the signature while exchange rates, collection systems, legal doctrine and court decisions moved around him.

For many emigrants, that is the hidden nightmare of departure. You leave to escape a system that has made ordinary life impossible, then discover that the loan, debt, family duty or administrative claim can follow you into the room abroad. Geography changes. The institution remains inside the envelope.

The foreign-currency-loan cases and KATA are not the same legal problem.

They rhyme.

A route is normalized. Many people enter it. The conditions change or the risk becomes visible. Institutions divide responsibility among themselves. The individual is told that the signature was personal. Years later, courts are still deciding how the systemic defect and the personal contract fit together.

I had believed financial independence protected me from that inheritance. I had no foreign-currency loan, no domestic debt and no employer able to remove my home with one decision. Yet the proceeding found another route toward the same fear: could the country that had contributed almost nothing to the reserve now reach the savings earned abroad and the home provided by my parents?

17. Beautiful Cities Can Still Produce Exits.

Budapest remained beautiful through all of this.

The Danube did not become less luminous because a tax portal was confusing. The Parliament, bridges, baths, museums and hills did not lose their form because citizens lost trust in institutions. A country is not ugly merely because people leave it, and departure does not prove hatred of home.

That is why the exit is painful.

Large numbers of Hungarians built lives abroad over the previous decades. The statistics do not capture every move, and no single cause explains them. Wages, housing, debt, language, professional respect, family, adventure, politics and belonging overlapped.

The London house was a collector of those overlaps.

Some residents lived more poorly in material terms than they had in Hungary and still felt less trapped. That contradiction makes little sense if migration is measured only in salaries and square metres. Predictability can feel like wealth. Being treated as a person before being treated as a problem can feel like comfort. A rule that remains the same long enough to plan around can be worth more than a larger room.

The opposite pressure is also difficult to photograph. It is the energy spent learning which rule is real, whom not to offend, which professional must be protected, which informal shortcut everybody uses and which formal responsibility will still return to you if the shortcut fails. One incident rarely drives a person across a border. Years of these translations can.

Part I told the wonder of the outside world without hiding the shared room. Part II tells the beauty of home without hiding why the system could make a returning person ask again whether he should leave.
