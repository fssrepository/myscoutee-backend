Because I was working days that sometimes ran thirteen, fourteen or fifteen hours while a tax system changed beneath a live foreign contract. Delegating the return to a professional had looked like the responsible act. The question became simple only after the delegation failed.

Did I have a loan? Was I working? What did I live on?

The questions kept returning as though ordinary debt and another Hungarian job were the obvious signs of a healthy life. In the London house, I had watched a foreign-currency mortgage help keep another Hungarian abroad while his family and obligations remained at home. I had spent years avoiding that trap.

After this sequence, taking out a domestic loan would have been one of the last risks I would choose. Returning to Hungarian work felt almost as difficult. The last time I had resumed work—especially cross-border work through a Hungarian legal route—the attempt had grown into the proceeding now asking why I was not working.

The reserve was not an unexplained alternative to ordinary life. It was why I could survive after ordinary routes repeatedly stopped feeling safe.

Why had I not sought psychological help?

Because I couldn't walk, I thought.

The body had a deadline. So did the return.

Each question could be reasonable on its own. Their order changed their meaning. The work, repair and operation receded; the person who had endured them became the object requiring explanation.

13. The Man the Report Invented.

The forensic examination took place on 30 November 2023. Two experts were present and the resulting opinion bears two expert signatures. The examination lasted roughly two hours and felt hurried.

The two people did not speak with one voice. One made the life-imprisonment remark before the causal sequence had been asked through and before I understood what material the examination was meant to rest on. Later, the other expert asked what had happened. I described the accounting failure, the pressure, the operation and the circumstances in which the words came out. His answer was the mirror image: certainly not for that.

Other remarks accumulated around the room: an “old man,” lack of success, the need for an accountant, the absence of psychological treatment. My later defence speech does not reliably assign each one to a particular expert. In my memory they hang between the two chairs.

The report contains the book's central contradiction. Its observations describe a person who arrived neatly, recognized the examination, remained calm and cooperative, observed ordinary courtesy, answered questions and was oriented to self, others, place and time. Comprehension and memory were intact. It recorded no perceptual disturbance, no formal disorder of thought and no earlier psychiatric documentation. It described my intellect as appropriate to my age and education.

Then the same document crossed an invisible line.

It said that my thinking was governed not by delusions detached from reality, but by false conclusions drawn from observed events and an unshakeable “global conspiracy.” It said I was incapable of experiencing positive emotions in human relationships. It described heightened suspicious readiness, hypervigilance, the assumption of hidden hostility in harmless remarks, easy offence and the possibility of an angry attack. In its final opinion, every offer of help could become evidence of bad intent; rigid beliefs, inferiority and depressive feeling were compensated through projection onto others. The diagnosis was severe paranoid personality disorder, reaching the level of pathological mental illness and moderately limiting responsibility.

Part I is not a clinical counter-report. It is, however, the missing human record. The man said to be incapable of positive feeling had spent years remembering moments of kindness across nearly fifty countries. He had trusted a WhatsApp driver in India, a stranger at a Russian bus, a local ride in Thailand, colleagues from several cultures and people who shared rooms, roads and jokes. He had designed a product around the belief that reciprocal trust could turn strangers into a group. The report reduced that history to social roles being destroyed by suspicion.

The phrase “global conspiracy” performed a similar compression. The account before the experts concerned a specific design failure: personal government credentials used as the practical substitute for delegated authority; notices routed away from the legally responsible person; a professional able to act under a client's identity; security exposure if stored credentials were stolen. Those were system claims capable of being tested. The report placed them inside a psychiatric narrative.
