The tax position still did not feel legible. Entries had appeared on the account after a return I had not known was submitted. The notice went through the representative channel while the liability remained mine. I had already spent days reconstructing a failure that had developed for months without my knowledge, and I was preparing for major foot surgery.

Under that accumulated pressure, one sentence broke loose. I corrected it immediately and continued communicating. The words were raw. The impulse beneath them was not: do not erase a person's dignity because the screens around him failed to agree. The sentence did not arrive from an empty room, and it cannot honestly carry the explanatory weight of the twenty years, the failed return and the broken information chain that preceded it.

The prosecution would eventually extract legal meaning from parts of that sequence. My defence would insist that the outburst, its correction, the lack of an operational plan and the administrative cause could not be separated.

The asymmetry is the Kafka mechanism. The system's failure was distributed across months, screens, representatives and offices. Its reconstruction requires a file. One person's breaking point fits in a single quoted line.

9. The Correction Does Not Trend.

On 5 December, I clarified the account by phone. I corrected the return myself and paid the tax required by the corrected position. On 8 December, I dismissed the accountant and terminated the representation so the three-way communication chaos would finally end and future notices would reach me directly. An official response later recorded the failed submissions, correction and processing history.

The tax problem was corrected. The tax was paid. The representation was over. The administrative cause had been repaired by the person who had not known about it until 23 November. A later tax record showed neither an outstanding obligation nor an overpayment.

On paper, the account had returned to zero.

The life around it had not.

Yet the broken sentence now possessed a different property from the correction. It could travel alone.

This is not only a problem in criminal procedure. It is a problem in every system that stores an event without storing the path around it. A star rating without the meeting. A rejection without the availability conflict. A warning without the later repair. A phrase without the exhausted body that wrote it.

Context cannot excuse everything. It can prevent the wrong thing from becoming everything.

10. A Body With a Deadline.

The prosecution later gave the monetary demands a central role. By January 2023, however, I had withdrawn the concrete demand. The tax had been paid. What remained was the fact that the only route that seemed to work in ordinary practice required a person to hand over the password to his government account. I had refused to make that bargain, repaired the consequences and still could not close the door behind me.

I documented the date and place of the coming foot operation, my request not to be disturbed during surgery and rehabilitation, and my intention to leave the country afterward. Before the operation, I called once more to make sure. The tax position was settled. Everything was in order. In return, I asked only to be left alone long enough to heal. There was no unresolved tax debt for my body to solve.

I had already paid the tax. My foot had been painful for months. The public system gave me an orthopaedic shoe and told me to return in a year. At the private clinic, they laughed at that timetable. I borrowed the cost from my parents and went private. After several medical visits, the operation took place on 15 February 2023. It was major foot surgery: the bone itself was cut. For roughly three months I lived mainly in bed, and the recovery continued for several months after that. My body could not be separated from every call arriving through the same phone.

I had gone private for the operation. The recovery was private in another sense. For much of that period I had to remain in bed with the operated leg propped high above me. A special elevation shoe protected its position when I had to move. Each day also required an anti-thrombosis injection into my abdomen. I was alone. Nobody came to look after me; food had simply been stocked in advance for several months. The routine was not an abstract reference to poor health: bed, raised leg, special shoe, injection, stored food, repeat.

Planning had stocked the food. Savings paid for private treatment. Discipline carried me through the injections. The chair beside the bed remained empty.

A call came in March despite my documented request for quiet. The repaired tax matter re-entered the room while I was immobilized and alone.

The return was two pages long. By March, it had been alive for seven months. It had outlasted my work, the tax debt and the operation. Now it had found the bed.
