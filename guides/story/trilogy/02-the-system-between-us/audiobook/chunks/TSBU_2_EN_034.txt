The 2022 timing forms part of the public background. Other operational programmes accounted for nearly €10 billion in EU budget funding—another part of the cohesion funds—that was withheld because the enabling conditions had not been met. The rule-of-law condition attached to this part included judicial independence. The wider framework also contained requirements concerning public procurement and academic freedom. The measures had no connection to the private case.

Also in July 2026, the Council of the European Union approved a new Hungarian recovery plan expected to allow €10 billion to be disbursed. Public reporting described access to the money as dependent on rule-of-law and anti-corruption conditions. The memoir uses the number only as an ironic echo of the 2023 postoperative line. It does not claim that the private sentence anticipated, influenced or caused the public decision.

A February 2025 interview records the justice minister saying that judicial independence was not in danger. Asked in a separate Index interview whether he had tried to persuade the National Judicial Council, Justice Minister Bence Tuzson said:

“This is not a Western. We do not operate on the basis of whose arguments hit harder.”

Later that month, judges and court employees rallied for judicial independence and the freedom to speak.

On 15 March 2025 (the anniversary of the Hungarian Revolution and War of Independence of 1848–49), Prime Minister Viktor Orbán said:

“After today's ceremony comes the Easter clean-up. The bugs survived the winter.”

The next sentence began, “We will dismantle the financial machine,” then named politicians, judges, journalists, bogus NGOs and political activists. The passage continued: “We will dismantle the entire shadow army.” Two days later, the National Judicial Council and the Association of Hungarian Judges publicly rejected both the suggestion that judges had been bought with corrupt dollars and the comparison of judges to harmful insects.

A separate 2025 controversy provides another reference-layer example of institutional certainty preceding completed fact-finding. In September, the justice minister presented a report saying that no minor victim had emerged in a children's-home case. In November, he said it still had to be clarified whether a minor had been involved; later proceedings continued to develop the record. This sequence is not part of the Founder's case and is not used to allege a deliberate lie. It is retained only as context for the period's conflict between definitive official language and unfinished facts.

Long-term work exclusion.

Eurostat defines long-term unemployment at twelve months and very long-term unemployment at twenty-four months. Its definition also distinguishes active unemployment from being outside the labour force. The Founder's approximate “nine of eleven years” therefore remains memoir language for time outside conventional employment, not a claim of nine continuous years in Eurostat's unemployment category. Longitudinal research is used only as population-level context for scarring, health and re-employment; it is not used to diagnose the Founder.

The 2026 transition.

Independent and official reporting supports the election result, government change, constitutional amendments, end of the President's mandate and interim presidency. The government's restoration account and civil-society criticism are both retained because institutional replacement is not self-validating. The public call for departures named the President, Prosecutor General and the heads of the Constitutional Court, Curia, National Office for the Judiciary, State Audit Office, Competition Authority and Media Authority.

The Ukrainian cash-in-transit affair and institutional departures.

The convoy sequence joins a pre-election enforcement operation to later institutional review, but the public record does not support one undifferentiated claim that every departure had the same cause.

The tax authority's 6 March statement records the two vehicles, seven Ukrainian citizens, forty million dollars, thirty-five million euros and nine kilograms of gold. It also records that substantially larger aggregate quantities had already crossed Hungary in 2026. On 9 July, the authority said its domestic inquiry had found no indication of a crime and was suspended pending an Austrian response; an internal legality and procedure review remained in progress.
