# MyScoutee — The Story

## Part II — The System Between Us

*A founder's memoir of rules, laughter, and the price of becoming legible*

> Review draft 0.1.0 — not for publication

<!-- pagebreak -->

## A Note to the Reader

This is a memoir-based documentary account of an unresolved period. It is not a
judgment, a medical opinion or a legal submission.

Part I ended with an invitation. I had returned from London, travelled, looked
for a partner, and spent the independence I had built on a product that might
help people reach one another more humanely. The last pages moved through a
Hungarian multinational role, COVID, and a cross-border remote opportunity. The
sentence at the threshold was simple: *what followed belongs to Part II.*

What followed is more difficult to tell because it survives in several forms at
once. There are contracts, invoices, tax returns, portal records, emails,
recorded calls, electronic submissions, medical dates, police records, an
indictment, a forensic opinion, petitions, political events and my own memory of
the rooms in which people spoke. These sources do not always describe one
another in the same language.

I distinguish four layers. A document can show that something was sent, filed
or decided. My memory can say how it felt. A later submission can record a
remark that still needs to be checked against audio. A public event can provide
context without proving the interpretation I gave an earlier private event.

The criminal proceeding described here has not reached the ending that belongs
to Part III. The prosecution and I disagree about the meaning of written
messages sent during an extreme period. I do not reproduce the most charged
language in this edition. It is enough to say that I wrote words invoking harm,
that I corrected and clarified them, and that the state later treated parts of
the sequence as a continuing threat. I remain responsible for the words I
wrote. Context does not erase agency. Neither should agency erase context.

Private people remain unnamed. Official remarks are attributed to my filings or
memory unless a surviving recording has been transcribed and checked. A smile
is only a smile; it is not evidence of innocence, hostility or agreement.

This is not a story in which one accountant, one tax officer, one police officer
or one judge secretly controlled everything. It is a story about what can be
assembled when a sudden law meets a low-trust culture, delegated access, partial
information, physical vulnerability, institutional delay and one exhausted
person who responds badly.

That matters because the pattern is not mine alone. Another person might never
write my sentences and could still enter the same corridor.

## Prologue — The Line Changes Meaning

At the end of Part I, the line on the screen was a network.

It connected people. Six strangers had become a group, a group had reached an
event, and an event had left behind people who could invite one another again.
The line was the path I had spent years trying to draw between intention and a
real meeting.

Then the same line became the ruled line of a document.

On one side sat the person. On the other sat a field waiting for a number, a
date, a legal category or a signature. The line no longer asked who might meet.
It asked what had been submitted in my name, who had received the notice, which
version was valid, and whether I knew what a system assumed I knew.

In Part I, I moved through countries. In Part II, countries collapsed into
portals, and portals opened into rooms.

A home became an office. The office became a tax desk. The desk became a police
scene. The police scene became an expert interview. The interview became a case
file. The case file became a petition. The petition eventually reached the same
European institutions that were being asked what had happened to the rule of
law in Hungary.

I had never been a party activist. I did not begin with a political programme.
When the KATA law changed in July 2022, other people went to the bridges. I
stayed at my computer and tried to adapt.

That is where the irony begins.

# Part One — The Legal Door

## 1. The Year That Opened

<!-- illustration: part_ii_founder -->

In August 2019, after years in which I had repeatedly wondered whether I should
return to London, I found a multinational role in Hungary. It seemed like the
kind of answer adults are expected to recognize: a known employer, a professional
position, a route back into ordinary life.

COVID made the apparently stable unstable.

Around the turn of 2020 and 2021, that path ended. A later position lasted only
briefly. I do not reduce those employers to villains in this story. The pandemic
rearranged organizations, offices, risks and expectations faster than many
people could name what was happening.

It also opened one unusual year.

Remote work made geography temporarily negotiable. I could live in Budapest
while contributing to work connected to a multinational telecom programme in
Brussels. The contractual route passed through companies in more than one
country. It looked less secure than the Hungarian job, but in practice it gave
me a more durable professional opportunity.

It also required a legal form through which the work could be invoiced. In
September 2021, I became a sole proprietor using KATA.

I did not wake one morning with an entrepreneurial revelation. I was not opening
a café, selling paintings or building a consultancy around a personal brand. I
was using the legal route available for cross-border remote work during an
exceptional labour-market moment.

That distinction matters. A tax form can look like a free choice after the fact
while functioning as the price of entry at the time.

The work was real and demanding. Some days ran thirteen, fourteen or fifteen
hours. Delivery schedules and production responsibility did not become lighter
because I sat at home. A system elsewhere in Europe depended on tasks moving
from one person to the next without an empty space between them.

I had spent Part I trying to solve empty spaces between people. Now my job was
to prevent an empty space inside infrastructure.

## 2. A Distorted Choice Can Still Be Legal

KATA was attractive because it was simple compared with the alternatives. That
simplicity also distorted the market. Employers and intermediaries could look
at a worker and see not only a person's labour but a tax route. The worker could
be described as an entrepreneur while carrying the dependence of an employee
without all the protection of employment.

I do not claim that every KATA user was a disguised employee or that nobody
abused the system. I claim something narrower: the category contained very
different lives, and the state had allowed those lives to accumulate inside it
for a decade.

When a route becomes normal, people build obligations around it. Accountants
develop habits. software systems encode assumptions. contracts are signed.
families calculate. foreign clients schedule work. A government can still
change the route. The manner and speed of change determine how much damage
arrives before the new rule has even been understood.

On Monday 11 July 2022, the new KATA bill appeared. On Tuesday, Parliament
passed it under an exceptional procedure. It was due to take effect on 1
September.

People went into the streets. Bridges were blocked. The public argument became
immediate: fairness, abuse, livelihood, consultation, legal certainty.

I was not there.

I was trying to understand what happened to a live contract, pending delivery,
invoices, foreign income, a closing business and an accountant who also had to
interpret the transition for many clients at once.

The law moved in a day. Understanding did not.

## 3. Protecting a Handover

My contract was extended on 27 July. That did not mean the problem had been
solved. It meant I was trying to gain enough time to leave without dropping work
that other people depended on.

By 3 August, I was treating the change as a force-majeure exit. I arranged the
handover and invoicing around the work that still had to move. I gave up the
remaining value of later months rather than continue inside a route I no longer
understood as safe.

From the outside, an invoice is a rectangle containing a date and amount. From
inside the project, it was time: work already performed, work still being
transferred, a foreign client's expectations, the accountant's interpretation
and a new Hungarian rule whose consequences were still moving.

This is where the story could have ended.

A normal transition would have closed the business, reconciled the records,
confirmed the final amount, delivered direct notices to the person legally
responsible, and allowed everyone to move on.

Instead, the transition created a three-person chamber with no shared window.

# Part Two — Three People, No Shared Screen

## 4. The Low-Trust Triangle

There were three principal participants in the tax sequence: my accountant, the
tax administration, and me.

Each had a different view.

The accountant could submit forms and receive technical messages. The tax
administration could process, reject, reclassify and call back. I remained
legally responsible, but I could not automatically see every delegated action
or every notice in the same form and at the same time as my representative.

The triangle should have been held together by trust.

Instead, the sudden law tightened every side of it.

I wrote too much because I was trying to preserve every condition. The
accountant experienced my messages as a scattered litany and signalled that the
relationship should end. Tax staff encountered a caller asking questions that
should perhaps have been settled before the call. I heard explanations as
responsibility being pushed back toward me. Every answer increased the need for
another answer.

Nobody had enough spare attention to rebuild the whole picture.

It is tempting to make one participant the villain because a story moves faster
when blame has one face. The evidence resists that convenience. The accountant
also sought help from the tax administration. Tax staff also worked inside
changing procedures and imperfect screens. I also made the conversation harder
through intensity, length and fear.

The failure was assembled.

## 5. The Password That Was Not a Relationship

The deepest design problem appeared before the final return.

In the established practice around me, some tasks could be completed only by
passing through the entrepreneur's own government account. Accounting offices
sometimes asked for the client's credentials. The arrangement could make work
possible while quietly destroying the distinction between assistance and
identity.

If I refused to share access, I could be told that I had prevented the
accountant from doing the work. If I shared it, I could be asked why I had given
away a personal password. If a representative acted, I remained responsible.
If a notice reached only the representative, the system could still behave as
if I knew.

This was not consent in any meaningful product sense. It was an improvised
custom holding together a formal process.

I began to ask questions that sounded technical but were really constitutional
in miniature:

- Who can act in my name?
- What exact permission did I grant?
- Which action requires my confirmation?
- Who receives the error?
- Who sees that the error was corrected?
- How does the responsible person know what the system now believes?

These are ordinary software questions. They are also questions about dignity.

## 6. Three Attempts at a Subtraction

<!-- illustration: part_ii_accountant -->

The closing return should not have been a philosophical problem. At its centre
was arithmetic.

Yet the documentary package shows repeated versions, corrections and rejected
or mistaken entries across several months. A return submitted on 30 November
became central. In my account, I did not know at that moment that it had been
submitted, and the form named me as a contact.

The absurdity is difficult to explain without making it sound invented. A
professional office, calls with tax staff, months of correspondence and a short
return produced several attempts at what, from my perspective, was essentially
a subtraction followed by a tax calculation.

The more elementary the operation looked, the more frightening the failure
became. If a simple form could not settle, what exactly was I expected to know?

I discovered the possible problem through an article and then through a portal
whose categories and identifiers did not give me one coherent narrative. On 23
and 24 November, I spent many hours checking. I called for help.

One surviving recording is remembered by me as the call in which panic became
audible. Other calls followed. The recordings still require a local transcript
before this book quotes them. My later filings record remarks that I experienced
as dismissive: that accountants usually did not cause problems, that
entrepreneurs did not normally care what accountants did in their name, and
questions about why credentials had been shared.

Whether every remembered word is exact is less important at this review stage
than the structure of the exchange. I had reached the institution because the
delegated route had failed. The institution's questions returned me to the fact
that I had used the delegated route.

The circle was complete.

## 7. The First Public-Interest Document

On 1 December, I did something different from merely complaining.

I described changes.

Send direct notice to the taxpayer when a representative files. Show the error
in plain language. Let the responsible person accept or challenge the result.
Do not require identity-sharing where granular permission would suffice. Record
important outbound calls. Pause ordinary enforcement when a serious health
event makes response temporarily impossible. Test new tax routes in parallel
before abolishing the old route.

Those proposals would become more elaborate later. At the time, they were an
attempt to turn pressure into design.

Two days later, pressure won.

# Part Three — The Sentence and the Body

## 8. Saturday Before Dawn

It was early on a Saturday, just after my birthday.

The tax position still did not feel legible. A payment or account item appeared
larger than the reality I believed I had reported. Notices, representation and
portal states did not line up in my mind. I was also preparing for serious leg
and foot surgery.

I wrote messages containing language I should not have written.

I corrected and clarified. I continued communicating. Later documents from the
tax administration reconstructed errors and corrective steps. None of that
makes the words disappear. It changes the question from a simple screenshot to
a sequence.

The prosecution would eventually extract legal meaning from parts of that
sequence. My defence would insist that an acute outburst, correction, lack of
operational intent and the administrative cause could not be separated.

I am not asking the reader to decide the criminal case here.

I am asking the reader to notice the asymmetry. A system can be fragmented for
months, but a person's worst sentence appears at once. The fragmented system
must be reconstructed. The sentence can be printed in bold.

## 9. The Correction Does Not Trend

On 5 December, calls continued around the misunderstanding. On 8 December, I
ended the accountant's representation. Around 18 and 19 December, an official
response described errors, attempted corrections and processing details.

From my perspective, the account had finally begun to tell the story I had been
trying to tell.

Yet the dangerous sentence now possessed a different property from the
correction. It could travel alone.

This is not only a problem in criminal procedure. It is a problem in every
system that stores an event without storing the path around it. A star rating
without the meeting. A rejection without the availability conflict. A warning
without the later repair. A phrase without the exhausted body that wrote it.

Context cannot excuse everything. It can prevent the wrong thing from becoming
everything.

## 10. A Body With a Deadline

In January 2023, I withdrew a large monetary demand and asked to be left in
peace before surgery and rehabilitation.

The operation took place privately in mid-February. The exact day still needs
reconciliation across my documents, so I do not manufacture precision here.
What matters is the practical reality: mobility became difficult, rehabilitation
required attention, and my body could not be separated from every call and
deadline arriving through the same phone.

A call came in March despite my request for quiet. More messages followed over
the next months—too many, sometimes angry, sometimes grandiose, sometimes
trying to turn private harm into a public programme.

At one point I sent the tax administration a photograph of my swollen,
post-operative leg. The image was intimate and ugly: a body cut open and still
trying to heal while the administrative sequence kept moving. I wrapped it in
an obscene joke about using that half-cut, swollen leg to kick an institution
in the backside because it was not doing its job.

It was physical comedy written from a chair, not a physical act or an
instruction to harm anyone. The joke was deliberately disproportionate because
the situation felt disproportionate.

The words were crude. They were also a visual attempt to make the body and the
absurdity visible to a system that had reduced both to fields. The photograph
could show what my explanations could not: the medical pressure was not a
metaphor.

I do not present volume as clarity. Hundreds of submissions can contain real
evidence and still make it harder for anyone to find the evidence. My intensity
became another layer of the system.

The larger my file grew, the easier it became to describe me through the size of
the file.

# Part Four — When a Person Becomes Evidence

## 11. The Knock One Year Later

<!-- illustration: part_ii_officer -->

On 22 November 2023, roughly a year after the first disputed sentence, police
came to my home.

The home in Part I had been the place my parents helped me secure, the place
that stood empty while I lived in a shared London room, the lower-cost base that
made independent product work possible.

Now it became a procedural location.

My phone and computer were taken. Objects that had held work, memory and the
unfinished MyScoutee system became evidence containers.

I remember using humour.

That may sound inappropriate. For me, humour was how a human being remained
present when every other available role was defendant, officer, device owner or
subject. A police officer could ask a hard question, I could answer in a way
that exposed the absurdity, and for a second somebody smiled.

Later, a judge could smile too.

I do not claim that the smiles proved agreement or bias. I claim almost the
opposite. The people in the room could still recognize the joke while the
procedure could not recognize the whole sequence that made the joke necessary.

The irony kept me from becoming only the file.

## 12. Remarks in the Margin

My later defence submissions record remarks I attributed to police officers and
forensic experts. They include questions about why I had returned home, what I
lived on, why I had needed an accountant, why I had not sought psychological
help, and comments about age, success and severe punishment.

Those remarks are not all independently verified quotations. Some may be
checked against surviving recordings; some may survive only through my account.
I include their categories because they show how quickly biography can become a
test.

Why had I returned?

Part I required a whole book to answer that.

What did I live on?

Years of shared-room saving in London, later cross-border remote work, an empty
Budapest flat and extreme restraint had made independence possible at Budapest
costs. I had never built a comparable reserve from Hungarian earnings. What I
had searched for in London, and briefly found again through foreign remote
work, was not luxury. It was room to breathe long enough to build something of
my own.

The timing made the later questions difficult to absorb. I had spent years
outside ordinary work, then finally found a lawful route back—especially a
route to work across a border—and almost immediately entered the chain that
became this case.

Across the eleven years after I left London, I spent roughly nine outside
conventional employment. That is not nine continuous years of official
unemployment. Some were spent searching, some surviving on reserves, some
travelling, and some building work no employer had commissioned. The usual
statistical language calls twelve months long-term and twenty-four months very
long-term. It has no equally simple name for a life that keeps working without
being allowed to feel securely inside work.

Research describes unemployment as capable of leaving a scar: return can become
harder, health and confidence can suffer, and employment itself can improve
well-being without erasing what came before. I cannot turn population research
into a diagnosis of myself. I can say what made endurance possible. I planned,
saved and kept creating. Financial independence did not make those years easy;
it kept exclusion from becoming immediate collapse.

The court later asked about my apartment and belongings. Now my government-bond
account has entered the discussion around my financial position. The apartment
came from my parents. The reserve behind the account came principally from life
and work abroad, not from the short Hungarian work sequence at the centre of
this story.

That reversal carries the deepest contrast. I had built security abroad so that
I could come home and live in my own country. When I finally dared to work again,
the resulting case seemed to reach backwards toward the home, belongings and
foreign-earned reserve that had made returning possible. This is how the risk
felt from inside the proceeding; it is not a claim that forfeiture has been
ordered.

No final confiscation or financial penalty is recorded in this review draft.
That distinction matters legally. It does not remove the personal meaning of
the questions. I experience them as the proceeding reaching backwards into the
only protection I managed to build before the proceeding existed.

That answer could sound suspicious if the listener saw only present
unemployment. From inside my life, it was the answer to why I had survived at
all.

Why had I needed an accountant?

Because the tax system and the contract route had made professional help
rational. The question appeared simple only after the help had failed.

Did I have a loan? Was I working? What did I live on?

The questions kept returning as though ordinary debt and another Hungarian job
were the obvious signs of a healthy life. Part I had already shown me the human
price of debt. In the London house, a foreign-currency mortgage had helped keep
another Hungarian abroad while his family and obligations remained at home. I
had spent years avoiding that trap, living below my means and preserving the
debt-free home my parents had helped provide.

After this sequence, taking out a domestic loan would have been one of the last
risks I would choose. Returning to Hungarian work would have been nearly as
difficult to imagine. The last time I had resumed work—especially cross-border
work through a Hungarian legal route—the attempt had grown into the proceeding
now asking why I was not working.

My reserves were not an unexplained alternative to a normal life. They were the
protective wall built because normal routes had repeatedly stopped feeling
safe.

Why had I not sought psychological help?

Because at the relevant moment I was trying to repair a tax record and prepare
for a mobility-limiting operation. That does not prove I needed no help. It
explains why the question felt as though the story had been reversed.

## 13. The Forensic Mirror

<!-- illustration: part_ii_expert -->

The forensic examination took place on 30 November 2023.

The report contains an important tension. It records that I was calm,
cooperative, oriented and cognitively intact. It also concludes that I had a
paranoid personality disorder and that my responsibility was moderately
limited.

I contest that interpretation.

I also refuse to solve the disagreement by inventing a diagnosis of the
experts. A forensic opinion is a document produced for a legal question under
particular conditions. It is neither a complete biography nor an insult that
can simply be answered with another insult.

The later political reversal in Hungary does not clinically disprove the
report. It does, however, complicate the public meaning of words such as
*paranoia* when institutions that appeared immovable in 2023 were openly being
replaced in 2026.

A prediction can be wrong, right, exaggerated or accidentally resonant. A later
event does not tell us which mental process produced the earlier sentence.

That uncertainty belongs in the book.

## 14. Defence by Accumulation

The investigation and court process created another triangle: defendant,
appointed defence and institution.

Lawyers changed. Communication opened and closed. Evidence requests were made,
rejected, repeated or reframed. Recordings and electronic records emerged over
time rather than arriving as one complete archive. The phone and computer
remained away from me for a long period.

I responded by writing more.

Defence strategy became public-interest proposal. Complaint became system map.
Closing statement became impact study. I divided responsibility into percentages,
then learned that percentages can create a false precision around a human chain.

The better formulation is not that government, accountant, tax office and
official each owned a mathematically exact share. It is that no participant's
choice explains the result alone.

I wrote the sentences.

The accountant acted and failed to act in ways documented by the record.

The tax system routed notices and corrections through structures I could not
read as one story.

The law changed with an extraordinarily short transition.

The investigation later interpreted the surviving sequence through a criminal
frame.

All of those statements can be true at once.

In court, the accounting failure returned in miniature. When I described what
I believed the accountant had done, the judge warned me not to defame anyone.
She asked why I had not asked the accountant. From the much longer defence
speech, she selected my assertion that the accountant had entered what I called
random figures in my return—figures I later tried to correct.

The warning landed with particular force because this was the same proceeding
in which a forensic report classified my personality in clinical terms I
contested and carried formal weight in the discussion of responsibility. I was
being told to protect another person's reputation while trying to defend myself
from a description that could follow me far beyond the tax return. The two acts
were not legally identical. Humanly, the contrast was brutal.

I experienced those questions as the system protecting its own version before
reconstructing the system around the person. The sharp phrase was easy to
examine. The two-page return, the repeated attempts, the outside help, the
missed messages and the fact that I said I had not known about the decisive
filing were easier to push into the background.

That is my account of the hearing. It is not a finding that the judge was
biased, or that the accountant committed an offence. It is the moment when I
understood that even my attempt to explain a failed process could be reduced to
the most accusatory sentence inside the explanation.

The hearing ended on two different scales. I said that European institutions
had already warned the government about unstable, rapidly changing lawmaking,
and that my case showed the size of the damage such lawmaking could create in
one life. The judge closed by saying that we would continue with my financial
position.

There may be an ordinary procedural reason to ask a defendant about money. The
emotional cut was still severe. I had lifted the lens toward legislation and
institutional design; the room lowered it to my work, debt, home, belongings
and savings. The system question became an asset question before the system
question had been answered.

# Part Five — The Earlier Pattern

## 15. Three Years With an Unlicensed Assistant

For much of the proceeding, I did not experience appointed defence as a stable
person carrying the case with me.

Lawyers were formally present at different points. That is not the same as
having one person continuously reconstruct the evidence, answer messages,
challenge omissions and explain the route ahead. My submissions record long
periods in which I believed I was effectively defending myself.

Conversational AI arrived inside that absence.

I used it to translate, impose headings, compare concepts, reduce repetition,
generate questions and find contradictions that a human reader could then
check. It made it possible to move through volumes that had become impossible
for one exhausted person to hold in working memory.

It was not my lawyer.

It could misunderstand Hungarian procedure, invent a citation, smooth an
uncertain fact into a confident sentence or reproduce the bias built into my
question. It held no licence, attended no hearing and carried no professional
responsibility if its answer was wrong.

The accurate phrase is **AI-assisted self-defence**.

For roughly three years, the same class of tool helped me build MyScoutee and
survive the legal file around it. In one window, I was asking how to preserve
human agency in a social network. In another, I was asking how to format a
constitutional complaint because I did not have dependable legal continuity.

From February 2026, the desk carried still more. I continued the public
E-közig prototype: a concrete model of documents, appointments, tax calendars,
representative permissions, direct notice and a customer-readable audit trail.
I continued MyScoutee. I built the books, prompts and moving-image plan through
which these two worlds could be understood. I also asked an AI system to choose
one of the six unsolved Millennium Prize Problems as an autonomous research
test. It chose Navier–Stokes.

The point was not that I had become a specialist in nonlinear partial
differential equations, or that the machine had solved the problem. It built a
versioned, falsifiable research chain, rejected sixteen intermediate candidates
and preserved a restartable handoff. A professor whose variational work had
helped inspire the experiment read two notes and the proof plan. He found the
ideas intriguing while asking the decisive question: exactly which mathematical
statement was being tested? My answer made the boundary explicit. This was an
AI-generated research experiment, not a verified proof, and I could not yet
defend it in a specialist meeting.

All of this work happened while imprisonment remained a legal possibility in
the background. I understood the charged offence as carrying a much wider
statutory range than the suspended sentence then proposed by the prosecution.
Neither was a final judgment. The point is not to turn software commits into a
claim of innocence. It is to record what mental survival looked like: I did not
spend those months only waiting to be classified. I kept making systems that
could be inspected, tested, corrected and handed to another person.

My defence speech expressly raised routes under sections 25, 26 and 27 of the
Act on the Constitutional Court. The trial court transmitted my complaint, and
the Constitutional Court wrote to me on 21 October 2025. It said the criminal
case was still pending and had produced no first-instance decision. Under the
route I had invoked, the complaint was therefore premature. The letter did not
decide the factual argument. It explained that the route could open after a
final decision if the other conditions were met, and that my attempted direct
challenge to the tax rule did not satisfy the exceptional route either.

The door did not say *wrong*. It said *too early*.

The judges then became another sequence that had to be kept straight. The
first assigned judge left the case, reportedly because of illness. I later
sought the exclusion of the second judge. A different judge decided that
request, called it unfounded and referred to the risk of a procedural fine for
an unfounded exclusion request. Even asking whether the room was impartial had
returned as another possible financial consequence.

In the communication around that decision, the challenged judge was described
as having listened to me conscientiously. I later answered the court in the
third person with a bitter inversion: yes, he had conscientiously defended the
system failure. The line was mine, not the court's. At the end of the hearing, I
read something less fixed in the judge's expression—as if he might have begun
to see a person reached by the failure rather than only a person accused of a
wrong. That was my reading of a face. He did not say it.

The next hearing had been expected on 2 July and was moved by roughly two and a
half months to 16 September. In my later account, the forensic experts had been
invited but did not appear, and the court said expert availability prevented the
earlier hearing. The exclusion request was also being processed during this
period. I experienced the timing as its own piece of black comedy: the system
had been able to turn an examination made soon after the search into a sweeping
description of me, yet years later the expert could be unavailable while that
description helped keep the proceeding open. I contest the report and its
premises. I do not present my account of delay as a finding that an expert lied
or deliberately obstructed the court.

<!-- illustration: part_ii_judge -->

Those were three roles, not one: the judge who left, the judge I challenged and
the judge who ruled on the challenge. From my side of the table, the substance
had still not been answered; the route kept changing its custodian.

My wider Drive now contains more than four gigabytes of material. Size is not
proof. It is the residue left when a person without a stable professional guide
tries to make every missing step impossible to ignore.

AI gave me speed.

It did not give me representation.

## 16. The House as a Human Archive

<!-- illustration: part_ii_housemate -->

The shared Hungarian house in London had contained an earlier version of the
same national argument.

Thirteen adults lived through one bathroom, one separate toilet and rooms never
designed for that many independent lives. We had not left Hungary for one
reason. Some wanted language, some work, some adventure, some escape, some a
second chance. Some no longer wanted to return even when the material conditions
looked worse from outside.

One housemate carried a foreign-currency mortgage behind him.

He tried to service the loan and public debts from London. Returning did not
feel possible. Separation stretched. His spouse and young child remained in
Hungary, and the family broke apart.

I keep the identifying details private. His life is not an exhibit against one
bank, one authority or one political party. It is a remembered example of how a
financial product can outlive the moment in which it was sold. The borrower
continues to carry personal responsibility while exchange rates, legal doctrine,
collection systems and later court decisions move around him.

The foreign-currency-loan cases and KATA are not the same legal problem.

They rhyme.

A route is normalized. Many people enter it. The conditions change or the risk
becomes visible. Institutions argue over doctrine. The individual is told that
the signature was personal. Years later, courts are still deciding how the
systemic defect and the personal contract fit together.

## 17. Beautiful Cities Can Still Produce Exits

Budapest remained beautiful through all of this.

The Danube did not become less luminous because a tax portal was confusing.
The Parliament, bridges, baths, museums and hills did not lose their form
because citizens lost trust in institutions. A country is not ugly merely
because people leave it, and departure does not prove hatred of home.

That is why the exit is painful.

Large numbers of Hungarians built lives abroad over the previous decades. The
statistics do not capture every move, and no single cause explains them. Wages,
housing, debt, language, professional respect, family, adventure, politics and
belonging overlapped.

The London house was a collector of those overlaps.

Part I told the wonder of the outside world without hiding the shared room.
Part II tells the beauty of home without hiding why the system could make a
returning person ask again whether he should leave.

## 18. Not Everyone Would Write My Sentence

To call the story universal requires care.

Not everyone facing an accounting error would react as I did. Not everyone
undergoing surgery would send hundreds of messages. Not everyone in a police
room would make a joke. The system did not type for me.

The universal part begins earlier.

Anyone can delegate a task and remain responsible for it. Anyone can miss a
notice routed to a representative. Anyone can encounter a law that changes
faster than their professional support can adapt. Anyone can be physically
vulnerable when an institution expects ordinary performance. Anyone can have
their worst response become more legible than the path that produced it.

That is enough to make the case public in meaning even while it remains mine in
fact.

# Part Six — A Private Case Enters Public Time

## 19. The Streets Begin to Speak

I did not join the KATA protest in 2022. Other citizens were already teaching
the city a different language.

Teachers, students and parents formed chains and marches around education.
Citizens filled public spaces during the presidential-pardon scandal in 2024.
Judges and court employees rallied in 2025 for judicial independence and the
freedom to speak. Other demonstrations defended civil liberties and the right to
assemble.

These movements did not share one programme, and they did not know my case.
Their significance was cumulative. Institutions that had often spoken as if
their internal operation was technical and remote were becoming subjects of
public experience.

My own path was quieter and stranger. I moved through electronic forms.

A petition to the European Parliament was declared admissible in January 2025.
The European Commission response described the data-protection framework and
pointed enforcement back toward national authorities and courts. This was not
vindication. It was a legitimate channel acknowledging that the category of the
problem could be discussed.

I had not become a politician.

I had become impossible to keep entirely private.

## 20. The Programme Hidden Inside the Complaint

Over time, my proposals acquired a structure:

- notify the person directly when a representative acts;
- replace password sharing with granular authorization and acceptance;
- make every filing, rejection and correction visible as one event chain;
- record important outbound calls;
- create a health-crisis pause without erasing obligations;
- simplify tax categories around invoice data where possible;
- let the administration prepare a proposed return rather than force every
  small worker to reconstruct data the administration already holds;
- strengthen worker protection so that a nominal enterprise route is not the
  price of employment;
- introduce new tax systems in parallel, test them, then retire the old path;
- use AI to categorize and explain, not to hide responsibility behind another
  opaque decision.

These are proposals, not proven reforms. Some may be incomplete. Some may create
new risks. The important change was personal: I had moved from demanding that
somebody repair my account to asking how the next person could avoid entering
the account in the first place.

That is political in the broad sense, but it did not begin with a party.

## 21. Four Years Later

In spring 2026, Hungary changed government.

The election gave the new Tisza-led majority enough seats to alter institutions
that had appeared fixed. The new government described its programme through
rule of law, judicial independence, anti-corruption and restored public trust.
Critics warned that rapid constitutional change could reproduce the same
procedural weakness it claimed to repair.

Both statements matter.

A system cannot restore trust merely by changing who controls the speed.

One sentence from my 2022 messages now acquired an uncanny echo. It had combined
an enormous demand, a period of twenty years and violent language about the rule
of law. In 2022 it looked detached from ordinary political possibility. Four
years later, public actors were openly describing a change of regime and a
restoration of the rule of law.

I do not call this prophecy.

I call it irony powerful enough to make time feel badly edited.

## 22. Two Letters to Venice

The constitutional conflict eventually reached the Venice Commission.

Tamás Sulyok, then President of the Republic, asked the Commission to assess the
attempt to end his mandate. His questions were dated 29 May and supplemented on
4 June 2026. He argued from the position of the highest office at risk.

On 5 June, I sent my own message to the same institution.

I wrote from the opposite end of the state. My submission argued that debates
about Sulyok, rule of law and human dignity should be read beside the way an
ordinary citizen could experience tax administration, police procedure,
forensic interpretation, clemency and years of uncertainty.

The two submissions were not equal in authority, quality or legal purpose.

That is precisely why the crossing matters.

The President defended the constitutional security of his office. A defendant
asked the Commission to look at the constitutional insecurity of a person below
the offices.

In July, a Venice Commission delegation met the President, the Prime Minister,
ministers and the parliamentary Speaker. Public reporting showed consultation
and caution rather than a final opinion that resolved every issue before
Parliament acted.

I cannot prove that my email changed the Commission's position. I do not claim
it did. What I can prove is that the same institution received warnings from
the top and the bottom while Hungary's constitutional order was visibly between
states.

## 23. When Seven Judges Step Aside

Venice was not Sulyok's only route.

On 11 June, he turned to the Constitutional Court for an interpretation of the
limits around constitutional amendment and removal. The Court had fifteen
members. Seven declared that they were personally and directly affected and
excluded themselves from the matter.

The full sitting needed ten.

With only eight available, the Court could not form the quorum required to hear
the matter, and it disappeared from the agenda before a substantive decision.

The Court had not literally lost half its membership. Nearly half had stepped
out of one case. The visual meaning was nevertheless extraordinary: the body
asked to resolve a constitutional conflict divided over whether it could even
sit inside the conflict.

This occurred while my own writings were trying to reach constitutional review
from below.

At one scale stood a President, an urgent request and a Court unable to assemble
itself around the question. At the other stood one citizen, a laptop and an AI
assistant trying to translate lived chaos into an admissible legal route.

The contrast is not that my document equalled the President's.

The contrast is that both exposed the distance between having a constitutional
question and obtaining a constitutional answer.

My own route had already met that distance. The Constitutional Court received
my complaint through the trial court, then stopped before the factual substance
because the criminal case had not yet produced a first-instance, let alone a
final, decision. The President's case was blocked because too many judges
stepped aside. Mine, as I experienced it, was stopped because I had arrived at
the constitutional door before ordinary procedure had finished with me.

These were not equivalent cases. They revealed the same architectural fact:
having a constitutional question does not guarantee a room in which anyone
will answer it.

## 24. The List of Offices

After the election, the new Prime Minister publicly called for the departure of
officials appointed to long terms across the old system. Reporting named the
President, Prosecutor General, heads of the Constitutional Court, Curia and
National Office for the Judiciary, State Audit Office, Competition Authority
and Media Authority.

The list was dramatic because it described how power can survive an election.
A government may leave while office-holders, boards, rules, foundations and
institutional habits remain.

It was also dangerous for the opposite reason. If replacing a government means
that every inconvenient office-holder can be removed through the same speed and
constitutional creativity once criticized in the old system, personnel change
can arrive before rule-of-law repair.

Events did not follow one identical route. The public-media structure and its
leaders changed. Sulyok's mandate ended. New age and term rules meant that four
of the fifteen constitutional judges then sitting—including the Court's
President—were due to leave on 1 September. Other offices faced different legal
procedures, political pressure or unfinished negotiations.

The correct image is not a row of heads falling in one cut.

It is a wall of institutional portraits, each moving on a different hinge while
one person below them is still trying to learn what will happen at his next
hearing.

## 25. The Empty Presidential Chair

On 18 July 2026, Sulyok signed the constitutional amendment that ended his own
mandate.

The parliamentary Speaker became interim President. At the time of this draft,
a new President had not yet been elected.

The empty chair is not my victory.

The election was not a verdict in my criminal case. A political majority did
not clinically disprove a forensic opinion. The end of one office-holder's
mandate did not establish every allegation made about the old system.

It did establish something simpler: the country had decided that continuity was
no longer enough.

Whether the replacement deserves trust depends on how it treats the same
problems when speed, power and inconvenience become its own.

# Part Seven — The System We Build Instead

## 26. The Product Returns

For years, MyScoutee had asked how people could be filtered without being
reduced to a verdict.

The legal story returned the question in a harsher form.

What should a system preserve before it classifies a person?

Direct notice. Visible actions. Consent that can be withdrawn. A record of who
did what. A route to correction. Private context rather than public humiliation.
Reciprocal evidence rather than one unanswerable score. Enough human space for
the next meeting to change the meaning of the previous one.

MyScoutee cannot repair a tax authority, court or constitution. A social product
also has its own capacity for harm. Ratings can become gossip. automation can
become coercion. matching can hide a judgment inside a number. local operators
can become unaccountable if the shared core provides no meaningful exit.

The lesson of Part II is therefore not that my product already contains the
answer.

It is that the product must survive the question.

## 27. Humour Is a Human Audit Trail

There is one quality no event log can fully capture.

In rooms where my future was being discussed, I sometimes made people smile.
The police uniform remained. The judge remained the judge. I remained the
defendant. Nothing procedural changed.

Yet the smile recorded that the roles were not the whole people.

Humour was not disrespect for the seriousness of the case. It was an informal
audit trail of humanity: proof only that, for one second, several people could
see the absurdity together.

That word was not added years later to make the story more literary. In one
2024 submission, I wrote that the frightening part was in the absurdity. In
another, I wrote that the irony was this: if everything had worked lawfully, I
believed nothing would have happened. Those were my contemporaneous
formulations, not findings made by a court or an institution.

The humour also appeared in writing. In one police submission, I ended by
declaring the institution fired. Elsewhere, as I remember it, I sent
institutions away to dispense justice in a pasture. A programmer with no power
to dismiss the state was dismissing the state in prose. The first line survives
in the reviewed record. The exact pasture sentence still needs to be isolated.

Neither line described a real transfer of authority. Their point was the
impossibility. When every office could redirect me but I could redirect none of
them, sarcasm briefly reversed the arrows.

Normal conditions might have prevented the error chain. A measured transition,
clear authority, direct notice, one coherent record and a body given time to
recover could have left no criminal story to tell.

Instead, the system assembled a tragedy with comic timing.

## 28. The Invitation Changes

Part I ended by asking people to test MyScoutee in real communities.

Part II ends with a harder invitation.

Test the systems between us before pressure tests them for us.

Do not wait for the frightened taxpayer, isolated worker, exhausted accountant,
overloaded official, defendant or judge to reveal every missing connection at
the worst possible moment.

Design the notification. Show the delegated act. Preserve the correction. Keep
the human being larger than the score. Let people leave a network without
leaving their dignity inside it.

I began this period as a programmer trying to remain legal and finish a foreign
contract.

I ended it—if this can yet be called an ending—sending institutional designs to
a government I had not helped elect as an activist, during a constitutional
transition I could not have planned.

Between those two moments, the proceeding moved from words on a screen to the
material remains of Part I: the apartment my parents had helped provide, the
belongings inside it, and savings built principally through work abroad. I had
returned because Budapest costs made the independence earned in London usable.
Now I felt that the price of trying foreign remote work from home might reach
back and consume the independence that had made the attempt possible.

The next hearing was scheduled for 16 September 2026 when this review draft was
prepared. A short reply from the new justice administration had recently
reached me, but its exact source file and significance still require review.
The country had an interim President. The final legal result did not exist.

The line remains open.
