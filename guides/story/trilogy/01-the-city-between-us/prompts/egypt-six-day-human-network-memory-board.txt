PURPOSE
Create a planning storyboard board, not a final video, for the Founder's
self-organized six-day Egypt journey after he had already returned home from
London. He coordinated the southern Nile route through WhatsApp, one driver,
local hotels and local people. The story is about improvised human
coordination, wonder, humor and boundaries under uncertainty — not luxury,
danger tourism or a claim that Egypt lacks systems.

STYLE AND IDENTITY
Use the approved Founder identity in the premium painterly cinematic 2.5D style.
Keep him curious, observant, budget-conscious and capable. Egyptian residents,
drivers, vendors, hotel workers, police and balloon crew are distinct adults
with dignity and agency, never anonymous exotic scenery. 9:16 mobile-first,
warm Nile light, deep desert shadow, believable scale, no generated text.

CHRONOLOGY LOCK
This trip happened after the London homecoming. Any use inside the film's
three-second world montage must visibly fold time through the cognac notebook;
never imply that Egypt preceded the return.

PANEL 1 — CAIRO AIRPORT, AROUND MIDNIGHT
The first Cairo memory begins around midnight, before Giza. A taxi driver makes
a personal claim that unofficial exchange would be better than the formal
counter. The Founder declines the expensive private ride and instead shares a
vehicle with a Sudanese married couple. He and the woman begin a playful
conversation while both partners react with visible amusement. As the taxi
pulls away, the couple's roof-stacked suitcases fall and turn flirtation into a
practical shared interruption. Hold one warm reaction tableau rather than a
slapstick crash. The woman is an autonomous participant, the husband is not
humiliated, the couple is not an ethnic joke and the driver's exchange claim is
not film fact. This is the first alternate to Panel 5 for the main-film Egypt
frame; use one or the other, never both inside the eight-image burst.

PANEL 2 — GIZA, WAITING FOR THE 08:00 OPENING
At the Giza Plateau, use the confirmed pyramids and camel experience without a
tourism-ad pose. The Founder arrives well before 08:00 but cannot enter until
the site opens at 08:00. While he waits outside, police carrying steel shields
stand ahead of him and stray dogs wander behind. Preserve this observed spatial
atmosphere without inventing a protest, attack, chase or threat. When the gates
open, the Founder enters and negotiates a price before the ride. Afterward, three
camel handlers surround him and demand more than the agreement; under pressure,
he pays. The conflict is the changed agreement, not a high opening price. Had
the higher price been stated in advance, he could have accepted or declined it
without complaint. He does not know the posted guidance or return to pursue a
refund. A helpful site manager notices his serious expression. One phone
photograph of the camel supports an improvised search; two stern armed
tourism-security officers seat him nearby while the handler is identified. They
later drive him back in an ordinary police vehicle. Asked how much should be
returned, the Founder names an amount almost by instinct; only later does the
remaining payment appear roughly consistent with the guidance. He would not
have pursued repayment independently and mainly wants the episode to end. Treat
this as one specific autobiographical dispute-resolution memory, not proof that
vendors are dishonest or Egypt is unsafe. No readable price sign, humiliation,
weapon close-up, chase, raid, monetary triumph or comic nationality.

PANEL 3 — NIGHT FLIGHT, ASWAN AND THE ROAD NORTH
Continue the first-day chronology. After Giza, the Founder flies south to Aswan
at night. The airport lies some distance from the city. Before dawn, a stern
guard stands high on an armoured vehicle beside a mounted gun at the city edge;
keep him distant, factual and non-spectacular. A police car leads a small convoy
through the desert toward Abu Simbel. Back in Aswan, a Nile-side hotel room looks
toward a beautiful unnamed Nubian island. The room has the scale of a honeymoon
suite prepared for two, but the Founder is alone. A clean WhatsApp-like planning
layer without brand or readable text then follows one driver north from Aswan
to Luxor. The car is chosen because several special sights can become stops; the
road leg is one long sightseeing day, not four days in transit. Keep Kom Ombo,
Edfu and Esna as confirmed visited stops in that geographic order. Human
handoffs, keys, calls and agreed times make the route possible. Do not claim
there was no transport anywhere, identify the island without confirmation or
turn security into action spectacle.

PANEL 4 — LUXOR BETWEEN TWO WORLDS
From a modest West Bank hotel outside central Luxor, frame two directions in one
visual sentence: rugged western necropolis landscape toward the Valley of the
Kings on one side, and Luxor's eastern-bank lights reflected across the Nile on
the other. A few serious tourism-security figures remain background observers,
not villains or spectacle. The Founder remembers a tourism sector recovering
from a fearful period; present that as personal atmosphere only. Confirm before
final use: Karnak Temple, Luxor Temple, Valley of the Queens, Hatshepsut's temple
at Deir el-Bahari and the Colossi of Memnon.

PANEL 5 — BALLOON IN THE VEGETABLE GARDEN
At Luxor dawn, a hot-air balloon descends unexpectedly into a vegetable plot.
Half the passengers exit through a private family home while residents, crew and
travelers cooperate to lift or guide the basket and balloon equipment around or
over a fence for removal. Use hands, eye contact and practical teamwork; the
landing is funny and communal, not a crash, disaster, invasion or mockery of the
home. This is the preferred single Egypt frame for the main film.

PANEL 7 — THE LAST-DAY SHARED BUDGET
On the final day the Founder does not want to exchange more Egyptian pounds. He
still wants a vase for his mother and tells the taxi driver exactly how much
money remains. The Founder—not the driver—proposes the practical constraint:
the vase is paid from that finite amount and everything left becomes the
driver's airport fare. The driver then accompanies him on foot through successive
shops to help him bargain. The first has damaged vases, so they continue to a
better shop. There the driver helps negotiate in a way the Founder could not
have managed alone as a tourist. The driver eventually holds his head in his
hands. The Founder tells the vendor that he is now bargaining at the driver's
expense; the vendor laughs and lets the negotiation end. Show one finite
envelope split between the vase and the car, a taxi driver accompanying his
passenger on foot, local negotiation and restrained humour. Do not show cheating
a vendor, fake currency values, desperate poverty or a guaranteed fare. Treat
the Founder's surprise that a taxi driver would accompany him between shops as
personal comparison, not a
universal claim about Europe or Egypt. Complete the route with two separate
Luxor airport security gates, then an abstract three-leg flight chain: Luxor →
Cairo → Athens → Budapest, with two connections. Treat the extra gate as this
traveller's observed experience, not a universal comparison or security
spectacle.

PANEL 8 — CURRENCY UNCERTAINTY WITHOUT AN ECONOMIC CLAIM
Merchants hesitate to bargain because the local currency feels uncertain in the
Founder's memory. Express this through paused hands, exchanged looks and a price
tag deliberately kept unreadable. Do not state a devaluation date, rate or cause
until the trip date and contemporary source are confirmed.

EMOTIONAL PURPOSE
The Founder is not alone because nobody exists around him. He moves through a
dense chain of brief human cooperation — driver, hotel, vendor, taxi, police,
family, balloon crew, travelers, a Sudanese couple — yet those connections are temporary and must
be rebuilt from zero at every threshold. This supports the later MyScoutee thesis:
preserve consent, context, capacity and the memory of real meetings so a human
path can continue. It does not claim MyScoutee would prevent scams, guarantee
safety or replace local judgment.

OUTPUT
One clean planning board with eight separated panels and no generated text.
The editor adds city names, chronology and CONFIRM labels in post. Generate no
motion and no invented landmark.
