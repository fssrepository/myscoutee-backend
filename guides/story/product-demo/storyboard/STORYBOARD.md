# 48-second product-reel storyboard

Every shot is one exact eight-second Google Flow / Veo 3.1 Lite first-and-last-
frame generation. The opening and closing branded frames are deterministic post
composites, not Flow inputs.

| Shot | Time | Exact endpoints | Retention job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S01 | 00:00–00:08 | `PR_CP01 → PR_CP02` | frame-one brand; a binary match still lacks time/place/plan; John demonstrates a private editable list |
| S02 | 00:08–00:16 | `PR_CP02 → PR_CP03` | the demonstration spreads; a different six-person user cast arranges six private lists |
| S03 | 00:16–00:24 | `PR_CP03 → PR_CP04` | reciprocal private priorities reveal one possible balanced activity group |
| S04 | 00:24–00:32 | `PR_CP04 → PR_CP05` | the group becomes people, transport, place and supplies |
| S05 | 00:32–00:40 | `PR_CP05 → PR_CP06` | the plan becomes a real meeting before 00:37 |
| S06 | 00:40–00:48 | `PR_CP06 → PR_CP07` | shared context becomes a warmer next invitation; closing brand |

## Opening branded frame — first visible frame

Use the approved binary-choice image as the moving plate. Composite the original
MyScoutee mark plus editable `yScoutee` at the top and `MATCH ≠ MEETING` at the
bottom. Hold only long enough to read—approximately 1.6 seconds—then fade the
overlay while the Founder moves from phone choice to the desk model.

## S01 — the match still needs a path

The Founder receives a familiar online signal without sadness or blame, then
faces two sepia portrait cards with simple reject/accept symbols and a phone.
There is still no shared activity, time or place. The symbols disappear while a
two-column by three-row private-list demonstration unfolds: John's portrait and
five distinct sample adults, each with a marker row, connect to one small table.
These five samples do not appear later. No named competitor, generated
interface text, mockery or public human rating.

## S02 — one demonstration becomes six private lists

John moves one marker. The six CP02 cards then turn to their plain backs before
leaving, so no face morphs into another person. A different six-user cast opens
in a fixed 3-by-2 montage. Each independently handles the fan of candidate cards
above their own head with subtle hand and facial movement. John and all five
sample identities are absent at the endpoint.

## S03 — reciprocal priorities reveal a group

The same six users stay in the exact same screen sectors. Each privately moves
one card in their own fan. The fans turn edge-on and lower without changing the
real faces. Six colored paths then illuminate one by one and connect the
reciprocal choices to six participant markers around a central table. They
remain physically separate here; no pair dominates and no person is publicly
scored.

## S04 — the match becomes a plan

The same possible group moves from private-priority evidence to exactly four
event-resource families: people, transport, place/accommodation and supplies.
The priority rows disappear, the six people retain their fixed sectors and each
begins one practical organizing action around the shared plan.

## S05 — people actually meet

The six separate windows merge into a real public room without identities
crossing sides. The same six adults sit around one shared creative/tabletop
activity with equal space. Reach the meeting image by 00:33–00:34 and let it
breathe through the observed 00:37 retention boundary.

## S06 — the next invitation is no longer cold

The same adults finish the shared activity and leave the same venue together.
Several conversations continue without forcing romantic pairs. The
chestnut-haired woman offers one blank tactile card for a future shared activity
to the whole group while the used table remains visible inside. Founder John is
not present. Fade in the exact mark and
`DESIGNED FOR THE MEETING.` during the final 2.5 seconds; end exactly on the
deterministic `PR_CLOSING_BRANDED` composite over `PR_CP07`. No operator,
administrator, server, deployment or business-model explanation appears.
