# 56-second product-reel source storyboard

Every shot is one exact eight-second Google Flow / Veo 3.1 Lite first-and-last-
frame generation. The opening and closing branded frames are deterministic post
composites, not Flow inputs. Repeated seam frames are removed after generation,
so the final picture lock will be slightly shorter than the 56-second source
timeline.

## Opening branded frame — first visible frame

Use the approved binary-choice image as the moving plate. Composite the original
MyScoutee mark plus editable `yScoutee` at the top and `MATCH ≠ MEETING` at the
bottom. Hold only long enough to read—approximately 1.6 seconds—then fade the
overlay while the fictional creator moves from phone choice to the desk pieces.

## S01 — the match still needs a path

The fictional creator receives a familiar online signal without sadness or
blame. There is still no shared activity, time or place. The choice symbols
disappear and the desk simplifies to one portrait card, one blank card and a
physical row of private position markers. No named competitor, generated
interface text, mockery or public human rating.

## S02 — one position becomes an editable list

The creator moves the aqua marker once. The desk pieces unfold into the larger
two-column by three-row demonstration above him: five equal-sized portrait
cards and one card-sized priority board. Each portrait row has one colored
position matching its path. The board shows a descending private order with one
intentional tie; it is an editable personal list, not a public human score.

## S03 — everyone arranges their own list

The complete CP03 demonstration—five portraits and one board—recedes unchanged
before the next scene opens, so no face or board morphs into another person. A
different six-user cast opens in a fixed three-row by two-column montage. Each
independently handles the fan of candidate cards above their own head with
subtle hand and facial movement. The creator and all five sample identities are
absent at the endpoint.

## S04 — priorities and context reveal a group

The same six users stay in the exact same screen sectors. Each privately moves
one card in their own fan. The fans turn edge-on and lower without changing the
real faces. Six colored paths then illuminate one by one and connect the
reciprocal choices to six participant markers around a central table. They
remain physically separate here; no pair dominates and no person is publicly
scored.

## S05 — the group becomes a plan

The same possible group moves from private-priority evidence to exactly four
event-resource families: people, transport, place/accommodation and supplies.
The priority rows disappear, the six people retain their fixed sectors and each
begins one practical organizing action around the shared plan.

## S06 — people actually meet

The six separate windows merge into a real public room without identities
crossing sides. The same six adults sit around one shared creative/tabletop
activity with equal space. Reach the meeting image early in the shot and let it
breathe.

## S07 — the next invitation is no longer cold

The same adults finish the shared activity and leave the same venue together.
Several conversations continue without forcing romantic pairs. The
chestnut-haired woman offers one blank tactile card for a future shared activity
to the whole group while the used table remains visible inside. The fictional
creator is not present. Fade in the exact mark and
`DESIGNED FOR THE MEETING.` during the final 2.5 seconds; end on the
deterministic `PR_CLOSING_BRANDED` composite over `PR_CP08`. No operator,
administrator, server, deployment or business-model explanation appears.
