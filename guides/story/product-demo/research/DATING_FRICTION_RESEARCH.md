# Dating friction: from discovery to a real meeting

Research lock: 14 August 2026.

## The honest starting point

Online dating is not simply broken. Pew found both positive and negative
experiences, and one in ten partnered US adults said they met their current
partner through a dating site or app. In-person singles events and professional
matching also solve real problems: they expose people to new candidates,
clarify intent, or put humans in the same room quickly.

The product opportunity is narrower. Across several routes, **discovery is not
the same as a mutually welcomed meeting with enough context to continue**.
The fragile section is the handoff: profile or first impression → reciprocal
interest → conversation → time/place/activity coordination → attendance → a
next invitation.

## What commonly goes wrong

### Swipe-first apps

- Binary reactions compress a person into a fast yes/no decision.
- Larger profile sets can increase perceived partner-choice overload; a 2025
  experiment found higher overload as participants evaluated more profiles.
- Users report uneven message volume, repetitive parallel conversations,
  ghosting, harassment and scam exposure. These experiences are not uniform:
  many people still report positive use.
- A match is only permission to begin another uncertain one-to-one negotiation.

### Traditional profile and questionnaire sites

- More biography and filters can clarify intentions, but long forms and profile
  comparison still describe people before showing how they interact together.
- A major psychological-science review found that access is a genuine benefit,
  while profile browsing can encourage comparative-shopping behaviour and the
  evidence for proprietary compatibility algorithms was limited.
- Regulators have documented complaints involving unclear total prices,
  automatic renewal, restricted communication behind payment, and some sites
  using fake profiles or paid chat operators. These are documented practices on
  particular services, not a claim about every traditional dating site.
- An inactive, misleading or paywalled profile makes the path to a meeting even
  longer, but a genuine detailed profile still does not coordinate the meeting.

### Professional matchmaking

- Human curation and explicit intent can be valuable.
- The trade-off can be a high fee, a bounded local pool, dependence on one
  curator, and disagreement about whether referrals fit the purchased promise.
- A 2026 UK Financial Ombudsman decision illustrates the size and subjectivity
  of this risk: the disputed international matchmaking service cost £16,750.
  One case is an illustration, not a market-wide rate.

### Speed dating and singles events

- They solve the offline transition and make romantic availability clearer.
- Speed dating deliberately provides only a few minutes of one-to-one contact.
  That can reduce awkwardness, but it can also recreate rapid comparison and
  first-impression pressure.
- Research on live events found that greater variety and more options could
  reduce proposals under limited time. Anecdotal complaints also mention
  attendance or gender-ratio problems, organizer quality and weak follow-up.
- Activity-led events are more contextual and may also produce friendship, but
  a ticket and a room still do not create a consented next step by themselves.

### Hobbies, classes and ordinary communities

- Shared activity supplies richer real context than a profile and removes the
  need to perform a formal first date immediately.
- Dating intent may be ambiguous; schedules, distance, cliques, repeat
  attendance and respectful consent remain real constraints.
- These routes are complementary to MyScoutee, not competitors to discredit.

## What MyScoutee changes in the workflow

| Common handoff | MyScoutee design response | Honest boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| quick binary judgment | private, editable `1–10` priority | not a score of human worth |
| profile or algorithm declares a pair | reciprocal/contextual weighted relationships can form a balanced group | no guaranteed chemistry or full group |
| every opening becomes a high-pressure pair | shared activity allows several conversations, friendship or later invitations | different people may still prefer a pair or another route |
| match ends discovery | each candidate accepts or declines the proposed group | consent must remain revocable and person-specific |
| scattered one-to-one messages | one contextual group chat and event draft | chat can still fail or be misused |
| “we should meet sometime” | people, transport, place/accommodation and supplies become explicit resources | software does not create attendance by magic |
| first date is a dead end | check-in, feedback, known contacts and later invitations continue the lifecycle | no promise of romance, safety or retention |

The group is not presented as a chaperone, quota, forced pairing or guaranteed
full table. It is a lower-pressure container in which a shared activity and
several possible human connections can survive even when no romantic pair forms
immediately.

## Claim boundary for the reel and book

Allowed:

- “designed for the meeting, not the swipe”;
- “a balanced group can form”;
- “everyone accepts or declines”;
- “the current software core supports this workflow”;
- “a later invitation can begin with shared context rather than a cold profile.”

Not allowed:

- “solves online dating,” “safer,” “better matches,” “ends ghosting”;
- invented users, success rates, deployments, revenue or testimonials;
- treating complaints about named services as universal facts;
- saying a group replaces individual consent, moderation or real-world safety.
