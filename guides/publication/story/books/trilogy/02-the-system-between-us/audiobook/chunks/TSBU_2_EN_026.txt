The humour also appeared in writing and images. I called myself “KATA's most wanted,” turning the tax acronym and the accusation into the title of a farce. In a Hulk-like image, a furious KATA figure held an axe in each hand while the tax authority, prosecution and court hung from his belt. A Hungarian pun changed the reaper into the KATA taxpayer.

Police recruitment posters supplied another impossible route. On 4 January 2024, I sent the police career office an English curriculum vitae (CV) and an inquiry saying that I had applied for the impossible position being administered at the station, had reached the third round and was still waiting for feedback. I asked for the earliest possible response. The accusation could progress through rounds while the person inside it could not obtain an answer.

Later, asking German police to help the unresponsive Hungarian route extended the same joke beyond the border. The humour was black because the need beneath it was real. In a low-trust process, sarcasm became an unofficial receipt: a way to record that a person had asked for contact when the official relationship would not reliably acknowledge him.

The joke did not deny that some sentences were dangerous. It denied the solemn fiction that the file's language was the only possible description of what had happened. When officers struggled not to laugh, the laughter did not acquit me. It briefly admitted that the institutional character and the human being were not the same person.

In one police submission, I ended by declaring the institution fired. Elsewhere, I sent institutions away to dispense justice in a pasture. A programmer with no power to dismiss the state was dismissing the state in prose. Years later, an election was followed by public demands for long-serving office-holders to leave. The old joke acquired timing it could not have planned.

None of these lines transferred authority or predicted a legal result. Their point was the impossibility. When every office could redirect me but I could redirect none of them, sarcasm briefly reversed the arrows.

Humanity had not vanished from the rooms. It appeared in an embarrassed smile, the second expert's different reaction, a lawyer asking for another room, a judge's changing expression and even my inconvenient attraction while my fingerprints were taken. The cruelty lay partly in the failure to carry those moments forward. Every person could remain human locally while the combined process behaved as though nobody were.

Normal conditions might have prevented the error chain. A measured transition, clear authority, direct notice, one coherent record and a body given time to recover could have left no criminal story to tell.

Instead, the system assembled a tragedy with comic timing.

28. The Invitation Changes.

Part I ended by asking people to test MyScoutee in real communities.

Part II ends with a harder invitation.

Test the systems between us before pressure tests them for us.

The test is not whether a system can find an individual error. Every system can do that. The test is whether it can still recognize itself in the error after the individual has been found.

Do not wait for the frightened taxpayer, isolated worker, exhausted accountant, overloaded official, defendant or judge to reveal every missing connection at the worst possible moment.

Design the notification. Show the delegated act. Preserve the correction. Keep the human being larger than the score. Let people leave a network without leaving their dignity inside it.

I began this period as a programmer trying to remain legal and finish a foreign contract.

I ended it—if this can yet be called an ending—sending institutional designs to a government I had not helped elect as an activist, during a constitutional transition I could not have planned.

Between those two moments, the proceeding moved from words on a screen to the material remains of Part I: the apartment my parents had helped provide, the belongings inside it, and savings built principally through work abroad. I had returned because Budapest costs made the independence earned in London usable. Now I felt that the price of trying foreign remote work from home might reach back and consume the independence that had made the attempt possible.

The next hearing was scheduled for 16 September 2026. The new justice administration had replied only that my submission was being forwarded for handling. The country had an interim President. The final legal result did not exist.

An older Hungarian instruction remained available: strive, and trust. I no longer heard it as passive faith in authority. I heard it as the right to try again, to look back from another world, and to ask whether home—and I—could do better.

The line remains open.

MyScoutee. Test the system between us.
