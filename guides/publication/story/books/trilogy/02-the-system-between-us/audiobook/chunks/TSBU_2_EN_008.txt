I began to ask questions that sounded technical but were really constitutional in miniature:

Who can act in my name?

What exact permission did I grant?

Which action requires my confirmation?

Who receives the error?

Who sees that the error was corrected?

How does the responsible person know what the system now believes?

These are ordinary software questions. They are also questions about dignity.

A proper delegated route would have let the accountant act under her own identity while showing us both every submission, rejection, correction and notice. I cannot claim that such a route would have prevented every later event. It could have stopped this particular confusion before it became the circle that followed.

In a functioning system, the distinction is almost too obvious to explain. The accountant acts under the accountant's identity. The client grants a visible mandate. Both can see what was filed, what failed and what changed. The person who remains legally responsible receives the warning before the warning becomes a penalty. Helping another person does not require becoming that person.

The formal representation route before me separated action from visibility. The practical custom of password sharing reunited them by collapsing two identities into one. I was therefore asked to choose between an incomplete lawful relationship and a more workable unsafe one. The system did not carry that contradiction. It waited for an individual to absorb it.

6. Three Attempts at a Subtraction.

The closing return should not have been a philosophical problem. At its centre was arithmetic.

The closing return was due by 30 September. The accountant therefore had months, not days, after the July change. By 23 November, it became clear to me that she did not know how to complete the short return she had been hired to handle. The same filing error had appeared in both my 2021 and 2022 KATA returns. She had criticized my reading of it; the correction eventually showed that my reading had been right. The documentary package shows three failed attempts, help from tax-office staff, unresolved processing errors and versions submitted without my knowledge. A return submitted on 30 November named me as the contact even though I did not know it had been filed.

The files did not arrive as one failure. They arrived as versions. One changed a figure. Another omitted one. A returned error remained in the accountant's channel without changing the next attempt. Tax-office staff helped from their screen, the accountant answered from hers, and I still did not know that the sequence existed.
