# From two books to an AI film: making THE PATH BETWEEN PEOPLE

MYSCOUTEE — THE PATH BETWEEN PEOPLE is finished. Created for the Higgsfield Global Film Festival, it follows a walk through an impossible London–Budapest, where streets open into rooms and fragments of a life return out of order.

Underneath the changing architecture is a question: **what remains of a person when the record cannot hold the whole story?**

[Watch the full film on YouTube](https://youtu.be/cA52DXZs5DI) — English narration, with Hungarian subtitles.

[Explore the project on Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/@myscoutee/projects/swipe-economy).

## 📚 From two books to three minutes

The film grew out of the wider MyScoutee project. A large source archive first became two books with AI assistance. Turning that material into a film meant choosing what could survive in a little over three minutes: travel, work, bureaucracy, people judging one another, and the small moments when someone made room for another person.

The story needed room for kindness and dark humour. One line captures the absurdity:

> “My fingerprints were more convincing than my explanation.”

A record can be precise about your identity and still understand very little about your life.

## 🛠️ How we made it

A few weeks before this production, I had little practical experience with AI video generation. Working with an AI assistant, I developed the story, references and prompts, generated the film material in Higgsfield, and assembled it locally with FFmpeg. The narration was generated separately. The footage kept its own music and ambience, with the voice placed over them during editing.

## 🎬 What worked—and what needed repair

Some of the camera movement was extraordinary. Architecture could bend into another city while light, glass and wet stone remained convincing. Those moments made the project worth pursuing. They also made the weaker results harder to ignore: a carefully planned movement could become a sequence of sliding rooms, or a continuous shot could jump abruptly between viewpoints.

Even the simple MYSCOUTEE lettering needed a local correction. A supplied final frame helped establish the next composition, but it did not carry the previous camera's speed, direction or sound into the new shot. Finishing the film meant checking those joins, repairing some of them, and accepting others within the remaining time and credits. Without external editing, we would not have reached this result.

## 📊 The effort behind the film

I kept a production log to understand the work behind the finished film and reviewed how closely the outputs followed the prompts:

- **49–50 hours:** combined working and waiting time for the recorded festival-film workflow.
- **28.5 hours within that total:** assistant task intervals, including tool and render waits.
- **12 sampled images:** roughly 90% of the scored main-content requirements were present; around 40% of finer detail was lost or changed.
- **5 sampled story clips:** estimated story adherence ranged from 30% to 75%.

The time estimate excludes the original master-prompt writing session, whose log was unavailable. The visual scores are small, subjective production checks, not general model benchmarks. They describe a recurring experience: the broad idea often survived better than its precise execution.

## 💡 What the tools could do better

Longer prompts did not reliably close that gap. I would like a video tool to tell me before generation when the requested events and camera movement are too dense for the selected duration, and suggest what to simplify. Discovering those limits through paid attempts leaves the creator doing work the interface could help with.

## 🎙️ Finding the voice of the finished film

The final creative challenge was the narration. A stronger text on its own could still arrive at the wrong moment or compete with the images. We revised it against the film that actually existed, keeping its meaning and dry humour while giving the pictures room to speak. The finished film contains compromises, but the question that started it remains intact.

The full film is on MyScoutee's YouTube channel, with English narration and Hungarian subtitles. I would be interested to hear which moments stayed with you—and what the images conveyed that the words did not.
