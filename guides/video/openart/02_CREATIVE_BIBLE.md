## 2. Logline

A man whose work at home offers no dependable future spends months on overseas
assignments away from family. An anonymous inspection-day boundary sends him
toward London, where five years of shared rooms, long days and deliberate saving
buy back his time. Nearly forty countries still do not make solitude belonging.
He comes home to look for a partner, tries digital and real-world routes, and realizes
that the missing piece is not another doorway to strangers but a bridge from
reciprocal priority to a small group and a real shared event. He gives that
problem his full focus, builds MyScoutee, then opens its next chapter to local
operators who know their own cities.

## 3. Theme and thesis

Theme:

> Proximity is not connection. A beginning needs somewhere to go.

Product thesis:

> Richer, editable intent plus reciprocal/contextual affinity can support a
> group and meeting workflow that a binary match alone does not provide.

Operator thesis:

> Software can provide the core; local people provide context, trust, venues,
> culture and continuity.

The founder does not stand above the network. In the final shot he becomes one
node among many, smiling because the idea has outgrown a single desk.

## 4. Narrative architecture

### Act I — Buying back time, then seeking a life (00:00–01:53)

Work at home does not feel like a future. Months-long assignments take the
founder away from family across Europe, Asia and the Americas. On an inspection
day, workers are told to remain out of sight; without naming an employer, this
becomes the quiet boundary that points him toward London. There he makes a hard,
rational bargain: shared rooms, long days and careful food spending for five
years so that rent does not consume the possibility of a home, a family and
future choice. He is not buying luxury; he is buying back time.

Across the larger journey he reaches nearly forty countries. Six to eight
illustrated memories flash by in a surprising three-second animated chain; the
beauty is real, but the final look-back remains unshared. He then comes home on
purpose: **“I left home to make a living. I came home to find someone to live it
with.”** He uses apps, attends real events, dances and goes on dates. The failure
is not effort, attractiveness or lack of people. Each encounter loses momentum
at a different boundary.

The first 113 seconds should feel like one unbroken thought, with hidden edits
behind a work-site partition, train windows, a shared-room door, travel images,
the returning front door,
shoulders, dance turns, steam and the founder's dark coat.
This is the formal echo of TSB: The Story's long moving historical shot. Do not
copy its character design, rural sets, color system, story or compositions.

### Act II — Draw a path (01:53–02:32)

The moving world stops. The camera finally faces the founder. A blank notebook
page becomes the MyScoutee loop. Flat profile cards gain depth; a binary gesture
becomes an editable 1–10 priority; two-directional lines become a graph; the
graph resolves into a small group; chat resolves into an event and attendance.

The pace becomes legible and deliberate. The audience should feel “of course —
the missing step was the path,” not “an algorithm saved him.”

### Act III — More authors (02:32–02:48)

The group event is seen through the eyes of a local operator/host. London,
Budapest and several non-specific cities light up with distinct local palettes.
The founder's notebook is passed across the frame and becomes many operator
workspaces. The final invitation is confident: the core has been built; now it
needs local authors.

## 5. Visual world

### Style statement

> Premium 2.5D animated short film: expressive hand-drawn characters with
> restrained facial linework, dimensional miniature/paper city sets, realistic
> cinematic lighting, tactile surfaces, shallow atmospheric depth, elegant
> camera movement, and a coherent painterly finish. Human, slightly imperfect,
> never glossy corporate 3D and never photoreal uncanny AI footage.

Why this style:

- it honors the emotional, crafted scale of the reference film without copying
  its design;
- character identity and gestures are more stable across generation tools;
- city transitions can be theatrical and impossible while still feeling true;
- UI and graph metaphors can enter the world without becoming a screen demo;
- subtle face acting remains possible without relying on flawless live-action
  lip sync for every line.

### Texture

- paper tooth visible only in highlights;
- wool, painted wood, fogged glass, wet pavement and notebook graphite;
- London stone and tiled Underground surfaces simplified into original shapes;
- Budapest tram metal and Danube reflections simplified, no official transport
  logos;
- no plastic skin, hyper-sharp pores, game-engine sheen or generic Pixar look.

### Palette arc

| Story phase | Colors | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| overseas work | industrial grey, travel blue, one family-photo amber | distance without spectacle |
| London saving years | slate blue, rain green, sodium amber, faded room cream | discipline and costly time |
| three-second world burst | truthful regional color inside one painterly grade | wonder without automatic belonging |
| Budapest return/attempts | desaturated terracotta, tobacco, winter teal, cream | familiar warmth, deliberate search |
| app overload | cold cyan/white notification light; coral only as a scarce signal | fragmented attention |
| insight/notebook | graphite, warm ivory, one amber line | clarity |
| MyScoutee group | coral connection line, warm amber, aqua, leaf green on deep navy | differentiated people forming context |
| operator network | every city has its own controlled accent around a common coral line | local ownership, shared core |

The final frame background is deep navy, not black. The original multicolor
`heart.png` remains unmodified; coral belongs to the connection line, not to a
recolored logo.

## 6. Character bible

### FOUNDER — protagonist and narrator

The final face must be derived from reference images supplied by the creator or
from an explicitly approved stylized design. Do not invent a “more handsome”
version. Preserve recognizable proportions and humane asymmetry.

Stable costume anchors:

- navy mid-length field coat;
- charcoal crew-neck knit;
- muted rust scarf in exterior winter shots;
- dark trousers and practical brown shoes;
- worn cognac notebook cover and mechanical pencil;
- compact dark backpack during travel;
- no visible luxury branding.

Performance rules:

- observant eyes; he notices the room before acting;
- shoulders begin open, tighten slightly through failed formats, then release at
  the insight;
- frustration is a quiet exhale, not anger;
- he never glares at women, couples, hosts or phones;
- humor is self-aware: a half-smile when the coffee machine defeats a sentence;
- the insight is not a eureka grin. It is a slow refocus, small inhale, pencil
  finally touching paper;
- final confidence is calm and direct, with relaxed brow and level eye line.

Expression sheet required before motion:

1. neutral attentive;
2. hopeful half-smile;
3. polite concentration in noise;
4. momentary social uncertainty;
5. tired but composed on the night train;
6. recognition/clarity;
7. listening inside a group;
8. quiet founder confidence to camera.

### MAYA — attention-overload counterpoint

Maya is not “the desirable woman” or a reward. She visualizes the other side of
attention scarcity.

- adult Londoner/newcomer; appearance derived from an approved reference or an
  original stylized design;
- moss-green coat, cream shirt, silver stud earrings;
- phone fills with generic notifications while her face moves from curiosity to
  cognitive overload and safety-checking caution;
- she later appears as one member of a MyScoutee group, listening and choosing
  for herself;
- she is never romantically paired with the founder in the film.

### DANCE STUDENT and PARTNER

- DANCE STUDENT: warm, focused, partnered participant; teal practice top;
- PARTNER: arrives naturally with an umbrella/coat, gives a friendly greeting;
- neither is antagonistic and neither owes the founder romantic availability;
- the founder's micro-expression is simply “context understood.”

### THREE BAD-DATE PARTNERS

They may be different people or one composite. Prefer three diverse adults so
the montage reads as format friction over time, not judgment of a single date.

- all show effort and basic goodwill;
- the coffee date laughs with the founder when the steam wand interrupts;
- the bar date cups an ear and says “Sorry — what did you say?”;
- the formal-restaurant date releases a small nervous breath at the same time
  as the founder;
- no one checks a phone contemptuously, spills a drink, looks grotesque or is
  rejected for appearance.

### FIRST GROUP

Five adults including Maya, varied in gender presentation, age within a broad
adult range, ethnicity and body type. Keep the group small enough for every face
to be readable. Their shared event is a low-pressure city walk ending at a
tabletop/community venue with a quiet conversation area.

Micro-actions reveal character:

- one checks the route;
- one carries shared tickets;
- one helps move a chair without being asked;
- two discover a mutual joke;
- Maya listens before adding a line;
- the founder is part of the group, not its centre.

### LOCAL OPERATOR

An adult host who knows the venue staff and checks the room before participants
arrive. Do not dress them as a platform executive. Give them practical keys,
tablet/clipboard and a warm, competent presence. In the network montage, show
several operators of different cities and communities, each with distinct local
branding around the shared core.

## 7. Location bible

### Budapest

- dawn interior near a tram line, first as departure and later as return;
- stylized yellow tram reflection, no logo or route number;
- Danube/bridge geometry seen as layered paper planes, not a tourism postcard;
- dance studio with timber floor, tall windows and old radiator;
- familiar warm practical light, early winter atmosphere.

### London

- no montage of Big Ben, red bus and phone box as shorthand;
- use lived London: early office commute, wet Overground platform, Zone 2/3
  street, corner shop glow, office lift, late workstation, escalator and late
  Tube window;
- one distant skyline glimpse is enough;
- shared bedroom is modest, maintained and respectful: suitcase, packed-lunch
  shelf and one private notebook corner; no misery-porn styling;
- an imagined independent rental may show why the founder chose sharing, but no
  invented rent, wage or savings figure appears;
- accents/cultures live in faces, clothing, food steam and fragments of sound;
- travel is shown through repeated door frames and carriage windows;
- long work is shown by changing daylight, emptied desks and repeated meals,
  never by humiliating the worker or blaming one employer.

### Overseas assignments and illustrated travel memories

- anonymous work site; no employer mark, uniform logo, country flag or readable
  paperwork;
- departure board and calendar express months away from family without invented
  dates;
- inspection beat is a quiet instruction to move behind a partition, not a
  chase or raid;
- Europe, Asia and the Americas appear first as an original paper-map route;
- no archive photos survive, so generate explicitly illustrated memories rather
  than fake home-video footage;
- the three-second burst can match-cut High Tatras → Schönbrunn arch →
  Neuschwanstein silhouette → Angkor Wat towers → Golden Triangle geometry →
  Norwegian weather → Mediterranean street → Chesapeake/Niagara water;
- hold each view for about 8–12 frames and use shape/motion continuity, not
  flags, souvenir costumes or landmark labels;
- the last view resolves into the founder's reflection and an empty adjacent
  seat, turning scale into intimacy.

### Bad-date locations

1. chain-like café, deliberately fictional and logo-free;
2. basement bar with too many reflective hard surfaces;
3. oversized formal restaurant where the table becomes a visual barrier.

### MyScoutee event

- independent multi-use community/culture venue;
- entrance visible from the table;
- quiet conversation zone plus shared activity;
- warm 3200 K practicals, soft daylight residue, acoustic panels/books/plants;
- clearly public and hosted, accessible route implied;
- five seats in a soft arc, not opposing interview pairs.

### Operator network cities

Use London and Budapest plus three original composite cities: Atlantic coastal,
Central European industrial/cultural, and East Asian dense neighborhood. These
are not claims of current deployments. They visualize portability only.

## 8. Recurring visual motifs

### The line

- Budapest tram wire on departure and return;
- London office timeline and rail line;
- phone swipe trail;
- dance-floor marking;
- coffee steam;
- Tube map line;
- notebook pencil line;
- affinity edge;
- walking route;
- operator network connection.

At first the line repeatedly ends at the frame edge. After the insight it begins
to connect people and places without tying or trapping them.

### Doors and thresholds

Every attempt uses a doorway. Early doors lead to another beginning. The event
door in shot S21 remains open and visible, symbolizing both invitation and easy
exit.

### The empty chair

An empty chair appears in the café, formal restaurant, London flat and founder's
workspace. In the event it is not “filled by the perfect partner”; it becomes
part of a five-seat arc. The problem transforms from missing one person to
creating a workable social context.

### The product mark

Use the unmodified repository `heart.png` briefly during the first 1.2 seconds
and fully on the end card. During the story use only the abstract coral
connection line; before the product turn, generic dating UI contains no hearts.

## 9. Cinematography bible

### Camera grammar by act

| Act | Lens language | Movement | Composition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | mostly 28–40 mm equivalent, occasionally 50 mm for faces | continuous lateral track, hidden transitions, mild handheld only on bad dates | protagonist often occluded or divided by architecture |
| II | 50–65 mm for attention, top-down 35 mm for notebook | camera stops, then measured dolly/overhead moves | lines lead inward; faces gain breathing room |
| III | 35–50 mm | rising crane and gentle orbit; no triumphal drone cliché | founder becomes one node in balanced ensemble |

Rules:

- no more than one conspicuous camera move in a shot;
- no constant push-in; reserve the strongest push for the insight at 01:53;
- keep faces at least 15% of frame height when expression matters;
- compose natively for 9:16: stagger multi-person depth vertically and reserve
  the top/bottom mobile UI zones;
- avoid extreme shallow depth on group shots; every participant needs agency;
- stabilize horizon through emotional clarity; mild Dutch framing appears only
  for the formal restaurant and resolves immediately;
- generate handles of at least 12 frames at both ends.

### Transition grammar

- foreground occlusion rather than dissolves in Act I;
- graphic match on line/shape in Act II;
- clean cuts on shared action in Act III;
- one short dissolve only: S08's last water memory into the solitary reflection
  around 01:03.

## 11. Acting and facial direction

AI video tends to overplay. Every prompt should use verbs and micro-actions, not
emotion labels alone.

Use:

- “his eyes search for the last word while the steam wand interrupts”;
- “she inhales to answer, then smiles because neither heard the question”;
- “his shoulders drop one centimeter when the pattern becomes clear”;
- “Maya reads three notifications, the fourth arrives before she finishes, and
  her thumb stops”;
- “the operator scans the entrance, checks the seat arc, then welcomes the first
  participant.”

Avoid:

- exaggerated sad eyes or tears;
- slumped defeated posture;
- finger-pointing, angry swiping or contemptuous eye rolls;
- generic continuous smiling;
- everyone laughing at exactly the same time;
- lip movement unless the shot has assigned diegetic dialogue.

## 12. Sound world

The sonic line mirrors the visual line:

- work ventilation → rail seam → room key → meal-box clock → eight travel hits
  → tram bell → app tick → dance count → espresso steam → pencil stroke → chat
  send → ticket scan → room tone → final two-note motif.

Music begins with one felt-piano note and bowed texture. A restrained 7/8 pulse
under the attempt montage produces slight incompleteness. At the insight, it
resolves into 4/4 without becoming a corporate anthem. The final lift adds warm
strings, low woodwind and human hand percussion, not a giant trailer brass hit.

The voice is close, dry and human. Preserve breaths. Do not use a booming
motivational delivery.

## 13. Titles and graphic design

On-screen text is minimal:

- 00:50: **I WAS BUYING BACK MY TIME.**
- 01:06: **I LEFT TO MAKE A LIVING. I CAME HOME TO FIND A PARTNER.**
- 01:53: **NOT ANOTHER MATCH. A PATH TO A MEETING.**
- 02:38: **THE NEXT CHAPTER NEEDS LOCAL AUTHORS.**
- 02:44: original `heart.png` + separate **MyScoutee** text +
  **DESIGNED FOR THE MEETING.**
- optional CTA under wordmark: **BUILD IT IN YOUR CITY.**

Add all text in post. Use the project's real heart asset. Do not ask a video
model to render typography or the final logo.

## 14. Ethical and legal creative rules

- Use only reference faces, voices, locations, music and logos for which the
  production has rights and consent.
- Tell supporting participants/voice actors how synthetic variants will be used.
- Do not imitate a living performer's voice.
- Do not use identifiable Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Meetup, TfL or venue interfaces
  and marks in generated footage. Generic visual language is enough.
- Research informs the story, but external statistics do not appear on screen.
- Keep an AI-generation disclosure appropriate to the release platform and do
  not remove required provenance/watermark systems.
- TSB: The Story is a structural inspiration only. No shot should be a
  frame-level recreation.
