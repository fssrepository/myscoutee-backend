### English performance and legal notes

- Soho, Tottenham, the Victoria line, thirteen residents, one full bathroom and
  one separate WC, and the shared bedroom are first-person founder details.
  “One and a half bathrooms” is production shorthand for that exact arrangement.
- The Founder already owned a home. London saving buys financial independence
  and time for his own work, not another house. The causes behind being pushed
  from his country remain personal testimony and must be approved before release.
- The shared Victorian HMO is a deliberate financial strategy in an expensive
  city, not a poverty spectacle. Show individual flatmates and their small
  personal worlds. “Buying back my time” carries pride and agency.
- The twelve flatmates were Hungarian. Their varied economic, professional and
  personal motives are founder memories rendered as fictional composites; the
  surrounding workplace, dating and cultural city supplies London's visible
  international mix.
- The 2012 Jubilee/Olympic arrival, Indian woman colleague, departing Polish man
  colleague and comic Soho costume-tail gift are personal memories. Keep the
  humor between competent friends; nationality is never the punchline.
- The shared-room resident swap, one-pound overnight coach to Edinburgh, Loch
  Ness, Glasgow/Clyde powerboat and compressed London–Manchester–Liverpool–
  North Wales–Cardiff–London circuit are personal memories. Use them as quiet
  visual or sound beats under existing narration, not additional VO. The Founder
  confirms every specific landmark before it is presented as visited.
- The adventure/crowding contrast carries the soul of the London chapter: a
  narrow private life existed beside a vast, beautiful public world. Do not turn
  either the hardship into misery spectacle or the travel into a tourism reel.
- The Brexit reference marks the founder's changing sense of work and belonging.
  It must not claim that migration generally displaced UK-born workers or caused
  low wages. Any argument about jobs remains background debate, not film fact.
- The spoken script does not list travel destinations. During its three-second
  burst, use only the confirmed illustrated memories in the creative bible.
- The travel burst is thematic, not strict chronology. The six-day Egypt trip
  happened after the Founder had returned from London; do not edit it as if it
  preceded the homecoming. A time-fold through the notebook can include it
  without changing the spoken return line.
- Lift warmly on “came home.” It is a purposeful turn toward relationship and
  full focus, not a failed migration.
- Dance and date lines carry observation and dry warmth, never resentment.
- Pause before “the bridge.” The product follows a relationship lifecycle:
  editable priority → reciprocal/context group of strangers → chat → managed
  first event → known contacts → later invitations/repeated events → evolving
  priority. Community is a possible result of repetition, not the starting state.
- Lowering a priority after conflict is a private change to future weighting,
  not public punishment, reputation damage or a claim of proven matching quality.
- Direct invitations belong after people have met and become known contacts.
  Auto Inviter may fill available event places according to the priority plan.
- The film may say the workflow is designed to remove missing coordination
  steps. It must not claim measured speed or superior meeting rates.
- Operators are technically capable and run independently hosted, own-brand
  local deployments. The shared-core server network/virtual-enterprise and
  opt-out are an intended operating model, not current-deployment or proven-
  efficiency claims. Reconcile exact legal/network wording before release.

## 4. Subtitle rules

- Make SRT files after the final edit, not from planning timings.
- Maximum two lines, approximately 30–34 characters per line in 9:16.
- Keep most subtitles at least one second and roughly 12–17 characters/second.
- Break at natural phrases; identify off-screen dialogue and meaningful sounds.
- Preserve a textless clean master for future languages.

## 5. 60-second cut

> I already had a home, but not the stability to build a future there. I wrote
> software in Soho and took the Victoria line to a crowded Victorian house in
> Tottenham. Thirteen of us shared one bathroom and one separate toilet. For
> five years I saved for financial independence: time for my own work. I came
> home to find a partner. Apps, events, dance and dates offered beginnings; the
> bridge to something real kept disappearing. So I designed MyScoutee to follow

> choices and context form a group of strangers. The group chats, manages an

> can invite again; repeated meetings can grow a community. If a relationship
> changes, its priority can change too, making another shared meeting less
> likely. Local operators can test one shared core under their own brands and
> cultures, cooperating through a voluntary server network with opt-out.
